Intel announced a $15 billion common stock offering on Monday, citing surging customer demand for artificial intelligence compute as the primary driver behind the capital raise.

The chipmaker said it will use the proceeds for general corporate purposes, which may include capital expenditures and working capital. The offering also includes a 30-day option allowing underwriters to purchase an additional $2.25 billion in common stock.

Intel pointed to physical AI, purpose-built silicon, and advanced packaging as the major growth areas it intends to pursue with the new funding, framing the raise as necessary to sustain expansion while maintaining what the company described as a strong balance sheet.

The announcement sent Intel shares down 3% in premarket trading Monday, a reaction consistent with the dilutive effect stock offerings typically carry for existing shareholders.

The move comes as technology giants have sharply increased capital expenditures to build out the AI infrastructure underpinning their services. Spending across the sector is on track to reach $765 billion this year and $1.2 trillion in 2027, according to estimates from Goldman Sachs. Amazon provided the highest capital expenditure guidance among major technology companies this earnings season, citing a memory crunch as a key constraint.

Intel's offering reflects the broader pressure on chipmakers to secure funding at scale as hyperscalers compete for compute capacity. Several megacap technology companies raised their AI-related spending targets during the most recent earnings cycle, underscoring the intensity of demand across the supply chain.

For Intel specifically, the raise signals an effort to position itself more aggressively in a market where rivals have moved quickly to capture AI infrastructure contracts. The company's focus on advanced packaging — a technique that stacks and connects chips to improve performance and reduce latency — has become a central part of its pitch to customers building AI systems.

Whether the capital raise translates into meaningful market share gains will depend in large part on execution. Intel has faced competitive pressure from both established rivals and newer entrants in the AI chip space, and the company's ability to deliver on its stated growth areas will be closely watched by analysts and investors in the quarters ahead.
