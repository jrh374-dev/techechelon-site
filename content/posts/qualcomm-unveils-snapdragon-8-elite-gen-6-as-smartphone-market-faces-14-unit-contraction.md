Qualcomm introduced its newest Android phone chipsets on Tuesday, positioning the Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 6 family as an on-device artificial intelligence platform even as rising memory costs drag the broader smartphone market toward a 14% decline in units shipped this year.

The launch comes as smartphone manufacturers grapple with skyrocketing memory prices that have driven up the cost of finished devices. The overall smartphone market is expected to shrink 14% in units shipped in 2026, and potentially another 1% in 2027, according to Counterpoint Research.

Qualcomm is releasing two versions of the chip, including one with additional capabilities carrying "extreme" branding. The Snapdragon 8 Elite Extreme Gen 6 variant can run AI models with up to 30 billion parameters, the company said in a press release.

Both versions will be built on Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co.'s 2-nanometer manufacturing process — the same node used in leading-edge consumer silicon — and will appear in premium smartphones from Motorola, Xiaomi, and ZTE.

Beyond the central processing unit, the new chipsets integrate a graphics processing unit and a neural processing unit designed to handle AI workloads, including agents running in the background. Qualcomm also added security features specifically aimed at controlling agentic access to sensitive user data.

Qualcomm CEO Cristiano Amon framed the launch as a platform shift, not merely a chip upgrade. "We're going into this transition from what is a very phone-centric model to now an agentic-centric model for new experiences," Amon said Tuesday at a launch event that was livestreamed.

During the event, Amon was joined by Google's Rick Osterloh, head of platforms and devices at the search company. Osterloh said Google's Gemini assistant will be able to understand a user's context — such as whether a person is driving — by drawing on the chip's processing power and onboard sensors.

Qualcomm is positioning devices running the new chipsets as an "AI hub" capable of processing AI models and generating outputs without relying on cloud infrastructure. The framing closely echoes Apple's language at its own launch event earlier this month, where the company described its iPhones running the A20 Pro chip as "intelligent personal hubs" for on-device AI processing.

"AI will now require the device to do much more than just execute commands, and it's going to be less about navigating apps, and it's going to be about completing tasks for you," Amon said.

Smartphone companies have broadly responded to the memory-cost crunch by concentrating investment in the premium tier of devices, which target consumers with greater disposable income — a segment where on-device AI capabilities serve as a key differentiator.

The Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 6 launch reinforces that dynamic, signaling that chipmakers and handset manufacturers alike are betting that AI functionality can sustain demand at the high end of the market even as overall unit volumes contract.
