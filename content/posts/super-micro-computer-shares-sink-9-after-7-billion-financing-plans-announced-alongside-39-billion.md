Super Micro Computer shares fell 9% in extended trading on Tuesday after the server maker disclosed plans to raise $7 billion through equity-related financing arrangements, even as the company reported it had received $39 billion in artificial intelligence server orders in recent weeks.

The company said it intends to conduct $5 billion in underwritten stock offerings and a $2 billion at-the-market offering, with the capital raises set to begin in July. JPMorgan Chase, Goldman Sachs, and Citigroup are involved in the arrangements.

Super Micro said the proceeds are intended to cover the costs of hardware component purchases, reflecting the intense capital demands facing server manufacturers as AI infrastructure spending accelerates.

Share price declines following stock sale announcements are common, as investors anticipate dilution of their existing holdings. Super Micro's after-hours drop came despite the company's disclosure of $39 billion in AI server orders from more than 20 customers over the past few weeks.

Super Micro is among a growing list of AI-adjacent companies turning to capital markets amid surging demand. Earlier this month, Alphabet, which provides AI models and cloud infrastructure for customers including Anthropic, announced it would sell $85 billion in stock, including a $10 billion investment from Berkshire Hathaway.

The latest announcement underscores the capital-intensive nature of the AI server supply chain. Super Micro CEO Charles Liang told analysts on the company's May earnings call that the cost of memory has more than tripled in recent months — a cost pressure that helps explain the need for fresh capital even as orders pile up.

Demand for AI-ready servers has driven significant revenue growth across the sector. Super Micro's revenue in the March quarter rose more than 100% year over year. Rival Dell reported that revenue from its Infrastructure Solutions Group grew 181% year over year.

Before Tuesday's after-hours decline, Super Micro shares were up approximately 39% for the year, reflecting sustained investor enthusiasm for AI infrastructure plays.

The company has also been navigating a separate legal matter. In March, Super Micro said a co-founder resigned from its board after he was named in a federal indictment over allegations of smuggling equipment containing Nvidia AI chips into China.

How investors respond when shares resume regular trading on Wednesday will signal whether the market views the equity raise primarily as a dilution concern or as a sign that the company's order book justifies the capital outlay.
