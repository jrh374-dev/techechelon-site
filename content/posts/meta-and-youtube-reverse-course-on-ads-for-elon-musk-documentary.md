Meta and YouTube have both agreed to run advertising for director Alex Gibney's upcoming documentary "Musk" after initially blocking the ads, reversing decisions that drew scrutiny over how major platforms categorize political content.

The Hollywood Reporter published a story Friday reporting that TikTok, Meta, YouTube, and X had all refused to run paid ads promoting the trailer for the film. The rejections were made on the grounds of "political content," a category that each platform restricts in varying ways.

Meta reversed its position within a day. When TechCrunch reached out to the company Saturday, a spokesperson said, "This was an error and the ads are being restored." Meta did not immediately provide further details.

YouTube also issued a statement Saturday, noting that "the trailer has always been available on YouTube" and that, "When submitted as an ad, it was temporarily restricted by our system. Following review the ad has been cleared to run."

X's refusal to carry the ads stands apart from the others. Musk, who owns the platform, posted that "Dogshit is worth more respect than Gibney," making the platform's position unsurprising. TikTok, which says it does not run political ads at all, had not reversed its decision as of Saturday.

The film's U.S. distributor, Bleecker Street, told the Hollywood Reporter it had appealed the rejections, and questioned how a documentary trailer qualifies as campaign advertising.

Gibney is known for documentaries including "Going Clear," about the Church of Scientology, and "The Inventor," about Elizabeth Holmes and Theranos. "Musk" premiered at the Venice International Film Festival earlier this month and holds a 100% "fresh" rating on Rotten Tomatoes.

Vulture critic Bilge Ebiri described the nearly four-hour film as a revealing look at Musk's tech career, his alliance with President Donald Trump, and his personal life. Interview subjects include ex-wife Justine Musk and former right-wing influencer Ashley St. Clair, who recently had a child with Musk and now describes him as "a nuke." Ebiri called the film "fascinating" as a character study, writing that "as a warning about our future, it's terrifying."

The Hollywood Reporter also reported that Universal Pictures was wavering in its commitment to distribute the film internationally, reportedly due to concerns about angering the Trump administration. Universal subsequently said it would release the documentary in theaters but was still working out its distribution strategy.

The film is set for U.S. theatrical release on October 9, meaning the window for promotional advertising is narrow. Whether TikTok follows Meta and YouTube in reconsidering its position could determine how broadly the film's marketing reaches audiences on social platforms before its debut.
