Federal investigators have cleared JPMorgan Chase of any external threat in connection with a recent service disruption, finding that a software release — not malicious activity — caused the outage, as broader market anxiety over technology reliability pushed Wall Street's volatility index to its highest level in months.

The FBI told NewsNation on Monday that it found no signs of malicious or external activity in the Chase outage, attributing the disruption to a software release. The agency's assessment offers the clearest public explanation yet for the incident, which drew scrutiny amid a week of nationwide technology disruptions.

The outage contributed to a broader sense of unease in financial markets. The Cboe Volatility Index, known as the VIX, surged nearly 5 points to a high of 27.7 at the worst of Tuesday's selling session, before hovering above 26 by later in the day. The VIX has now climbed for four consecutive days and has not traded below 16 since October 16, according to market data.

The S&P 500 slipped below 7,300, its lowest level since June, reinforcing the view that the anxiety extended well beyond any single sector.

Traders piled into protective put options on the S&P 500 Index at an elevated rate Tuesday, with the ratio of put to call volumes on the SPDR S&P 500 ETF hitting 1.5 — the highest since the first week of April, according to Barchart data. Cboe's three-month volatility gauge, VIX3M, briefly touched its highest level since April.

"Today, the market is pricing acute near-term stress in large-caps broadly, but tech-specific fear is still notable," said Eric Metz, CIO of SpiderRock Advisors for BlackRock, in an emailed statement.

The sell-off carried a broader signature than a typical technology correction. Volatility in the S&P 500 rose faster than in the Nasdaq 100 on Tuesday, a divergence that analysts say points to market-wide stress rather than a narrowly AI-driven pullback.

"There's a lot of reasons to be risk-off," said Noel Smith, co-CIO of Kai Asset Management. "This is a market-wide phenomenon today that's not just AI stocks."

Software stocks were among the hardest hit, declining ahead of Anthropic's initial public offering. Options volume on the iShares Expanded Tech-Software Sector ETF surged to nearly four times its 30-day average, according to Cboe LiveVol data. Salesforce was the most notable laggard in the group, with put contracts outpacing calls on twice the average options volume.

Nvidia's upcoming earnings report added another layer of uncertainty. Options on the stock currently imply a move of more than 7% following the report — the largest implied swing in nearly two years.

Not all market participants read Tuesday's conditions as a full-scale retreat. "While vol is now at a relatively elevated level, we have not seen much fear in the options market," said VolSignals' Matthew Nadel, noting that the move higher in S&P 500 fixed-strike volatility had been "mostly orderly." The Nasdaq 100 also held its November low during the session, and the VIX remained below its November 5 high of 28.4.

Adding a regulatory dimension to the week's volatility, prediction market platform Kalshi confirmed it had halted markets allowing users to trade on what President Trump might say on specific dates. The company said the Commodity Futures Trading Commission issued an order on Friday to pause the Trump-related mention markets, and that it was "complying with the order."

With the FBI's findings reducing the risk of a cybersecurity escalation around the Chase disruption, attention turns to whether the confluence of Nvidia earnings, Anthropic's IPO, and ongoing questions about tech reliability will sustain elevated volatility or prove to be a short-lived spike.
