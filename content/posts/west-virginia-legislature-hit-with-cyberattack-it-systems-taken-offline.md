West Virginia's state legislature is responding to a cyberattack that knocked its website offline and disrupted internal IT systems, officials said Friday, amid a broader surge in ransomware incidents targeting government bodies across the United States.

Ann Ali, deputy chief of staff for the House of Delegates, told Recorded Future News that the attack began Thursday and continued through Friday, prompting an immediate response from internal and outside cybersecurity specialists.

"Upon discovery, the relevant teams immediately took steps to contain the incident and began an investigation with the help of outside cybersecurity specialists," Ali said. "In connection with the investigation, access to certain Legislature IT systems has been temporarily disabled. However, based on what we know so far, the incident appears to be limited to the Legislature's computer systems."

Ali confirmed that no other state agencies or offices were affected by the attack.

The investigation remains ongoing. Ali did not respond to follow-up questions about whether the incident was a ransomware attack, and no hacking groups had claimed responsibility as of Friday.

The West Virginia legislature comprises two bodies — a Senate and a House of Delegates — with 134 total members representing the state's 1.7 million residents.

The attack comes as ransomware incidents against government institutions have climbed sharply. Security research firm Comparitech tracked 132 ransomware attacks on government bodies across the United States in the third quarter alone — a 91% increase compared to the same period last year. At least 41 of those attacks involved U.S.-based municipalities.

West Virginia has seen similar incidents before. The cities of Beckley and Westmoreland have both dealt with ransomware attacks in recent years.

The pace of attacks has not slowed this week. Residents of South Portland, Maine, lost access to critical city services due to a cyberattack, while Minnesota's Otter Tail County notified its 61,000 residents of a data breach caused by a ransomware attack that occurred in July.

A separate high-profile incident this quarter struck Anne Arundel County in Maryland, underscoring the continued vulnerability of state and local government infrastructure to intrusions.

With an investigation still active and no attribution made, the full scope of the West Virginia breach — including whether any data was exfiltrated — remains unclear, and authorities have not indicated when affected systems will be restored to full operation.
