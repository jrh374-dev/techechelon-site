Apple reported Q3 revenue of $89.5 billion on Thursday, $2.4 billion above consensus, with iPhone shipments in greater Asia rising 12 percent year-over-year. Services, the company's now-largest gross profit contributor, grew 14 percent and crossed $26 billion for the quarter.

The Asia number is the only one that mattered to the buy side. iPhone has been a flat-to-declining story in North America and Europe for two years, and the bull case has explicitly rested on Apple closing the gap with local Chinese competitors. This quarter says they did.

## Services keeps eating

CFO Luca Maestri spent most of the call talking about services. App Store revenue grew double-digits, Apple Pay processed a record $89 billion in transit transactions, and the company's services gross margin ticked above 75 percent for the first time. The structural shift from a hardware company to a services-attached hardware company is now nearly complete.

The stock was up 3.4 percent in after-hours trading. Analysts at Wedbush and Morgan Stanley both raised their price targets within an hour of the print.
