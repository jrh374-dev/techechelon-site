CrowdStrike is in discussions to acquire fellow cybersecurity firm Zscaler, a potential deal that would rank among the largest transactions in the history of the industry, according to a report citing people familiar with the matter.

Zscaler shares surged 18% in extended trading Friday following the report. CrowdStrike shares fell more than 8% on the news.

CrowdStrike declined to comment on the report. Zscaler did not immediately respond to requests for comment. The talks between the two companies are described as ongoing.

CrowdStrike currently carries a market capitalization of $235 billion, while Zscaler's market cap stands at approximately $32 billion — down roughly 13% for the year.

Should a transaction close at or near Zscaler's current valuation, it would exceed several of the most notable recent deals in the sector. Google acquired cloud security firm Wiz last year for $32 billion. In 2024, Cisco paid approximately $28 billion for Splunk. Earlier this year, Palo Alto Networks closed its purchase of CyberArk for $25 billion.

CrowdStrike's prior acquisitions have been considerably smaller in scale. The company announced plans earlier this month to acquire agentic observability platform Fiddler AI and AI trust platform Dreadnode, though financial terms of those deals were not disclosed.

The reported talks come as CrowdStrike has been among the standout performers in the cybersecurity sector during the artificial intelligence era, with its stock approximately doubling in value this year. Zscaler, a cloud-native security vendor known for its zero-trust network access architecture, has moved in the opposite direction over the same period.

The broader cybersecurity landscape has benefited from AI-driven enterprise spending, with companies racing to upgrade network defenses. Palo Alto Networks, another major player in the space, closed Friday at a market capitalization of nearly $282 billion, underscoring how the sector's leaders have widened their valuations relative to smaller peers.

A combined CrowdStrike-Zscaler entity would bring together two distinct security approaches — CrowdStrike's endpoint and threat intelligence platform and Zscaler's cloud-delivered network security — under a single vendor at a scale that would significantly alter the competitive dynamics of the market.

Analysts and investors will be watching closely for any official confirmation or breakdown of the discussions, as well as any indication of deal structure and financing, given the size of the potential transaction relative to CrowdStrike's existing acquisition history.
