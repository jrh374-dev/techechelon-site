Anthropic, the artificial intelligence company behind the Claude family of models, is set to price its initial public offering on November 18 and begin trading the following day, in what would rank among the largest public market debuts in technology history.

In updated IPO paperwork filed Tuesday, Anthropic outlined plans to offer 825 million shares at $70 to $78 apiece. At the high end of that range, the offering would raise $64.35 billion and value the company at up to $1.405 trillion on a fully diluted basis.

Much of Anthropic's recent revenue growth has been attributed to Claude Code, its AI coding agent, which has gained traction among enterprise customers. The company has also introduced Claude Cowork, a suite of industry-specific tools designed to compete with Microsoft Office.

Anthropic carries roughly $900 billion in future computing commitments, a figure that underscores the capital intensity of operating at the frontier of large-scale AI development.

The company has recently cut prices on several Claude models, raising questions about whether customers are becoming more cost-conscious or shifting toward lower-cost alternatives. Revenue growth remained strong in October despite those reductions, according to available data, though the durability of that momentum is still being assessed by analysts.

Anthropic has built its public identity around a commitment to AI safety. CEO Dario Amodei has repeatedly warned about the potential dangers posed by increasingly powerful AI systems. Some analysts say that posture could constrain revenue growth if it leads to slower model releases, while others argue a safety-first reputation could prove a competitive advantage as enterprises grow more cautious about deploying autonomous AI.

Jim Cramer, host of CNBC's "Mad Money," outlined three uncertainties shaping his evaluation of the offering on Thursday: the long-term impact of Anthropic's price cuts on revenue, the question of where the company's next $100 billion in revenue will come from, and whether its AI safety focus could eventually limit growth. "For now, I'll just tell you that I'm wary of doing so," Cramer said, referring to buying shares at the open.

Cramer pointed to cybersecurity, life sciences, and robotics as potential future growth vectors for Anthropic, beyond its current enterprise coding and productivity offerings.

The IPO arrives amid turbulence in broader technology markets. Nvidia shares fell 4.3% on Thursday following the resignation of founder and CEO Jensen Huang, dragging down the Nasdaq Composite and S&P 500 by roughly 0.4% each. The sell-off adds a note of caution to what would otherwise be a favorable backdrop for a high-profile AI listing.

Whether Anthropic's safety-centric positioning and rapid growth are sufficient to justify a valuation in excess of $1.4 trillion will likely be tested in the days following its November 19 trading debut.
