Shares of CrowdStrike and Palo Alto Networks each jumped more than 5% to record highs on Monday, following the cybersecurity industry's annual Black Hat conference in Las Vegas, where AI-driven threats dominated discussions among executives, vendors, and customers.

The rally came as analysts returned from the conference with a unified assessment: the threat landscape has shifted in a fundamental way.

"The single most consistent theme across our conversations — partners, vendors, and customers alike — was that AI agents have fundamentally changed the threat landscape," analysts at BTIG wrote in a note to clients following the event.

BTIG characterized the security environment as "meaningfully worse," while noting that deployment of AI security tools remains in early stages. Businesses are increasingly turning to cybersecurity vendors for agentic tools capable of fending off adversaries in what analysts describe as a hyper-accelerated threat environment fueled by new AI-based attack models.

Analysts at Cantor echoed that view. "AI has moved from being a cybersecurity feature to a key pillar of both the attack surface and the attacker/defender infrastructure," the firm wrote.

BTIG raised its price target on Palo Alto Networks to $380 per share, reflecting approximately 4% upside from Friday's close. The firm cited Palo Alto's identity platform as a direct beneficiary of AI agent proliferation, and pointed to products including XSIAM and Chronosphere as forming a "data moat" across other security verticals.

The firm also lifted its price target on CrowdStrike to $237 per share, representing 11% upside from Friday's close. "We think AI is creating a new modernization cycle in the endpoint security space, which directly benefits CRWD's core business," BTIG wrote. The firm additionally boosted its target on Rubrik to $109 per share.

The broader cybersecurity sector moved with the two headliners. Tenable and Rubrik each climbed more than 7%, while Netskope and Zscaler rose approximately 5% each.

The conference-driven momentum underscores a growing conviction among security buyers and investors that AI-native defense tools are transitioning from optional upgrades to necessary infrastructure. Executives gathered in Las Vegas specifically seeking solutions to secure systems against rogue AI agents — a threat category that has risen sharply in industry concern over the past year.

With AI agents now regarded as the predominant attack vector and analysts across multiple firms flagging only early-stage adoption of countermeasures, the sector appears positioned for a sustained spending cycle — one that could keep both CrowdStrike and Palo Alto at the center of enterprise security budgets heading into 2027.
