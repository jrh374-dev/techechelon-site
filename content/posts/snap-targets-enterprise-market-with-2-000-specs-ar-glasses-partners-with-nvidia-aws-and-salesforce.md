Snap is pushing its $2,000 Specs augmented reality glasses into the enterprise market, announcing partnerships with Nvidia, Amazon Web Services, and Salesforce as the social media company attempts to diversify beyond its consumer roots.

CEO Evan Spiegel framed the initiative as an effort to help businesses "understand that Specs is a computer, not a pair of smart glasses for taking photos," signaling that the company views the device as a serious productivity tool rather than a consumer novelty.

Snap first unveiled the Specs AR glasses in June at a $2,195 price tag with a $200 refundable deposit, initially positioning the device for general consumers rather than developers or business users. The new enterprise push represents a pivot in go-to-market strategy, coming roughly three months after that launch.

The partnerships are designed to give businesses easier access to existing AI development ecosystems. On the Nvidia side, the arrangement centers on Nvidia's open-source XR AI platform, which Spiegel said a number of businesses are already building on.

"The goal with both Salesforce and Nvidia is to make it really easy for businesses to get the benefits of those agentic platforms," Spiegel said.

Spiegel pointed to field service work as one concrete use case — technicians troubleshooting equipment in the field by viewing digital instructions overlaid on the physical world and interacting with remote colleagues, tasks that he argued older, bulkier devices were ill-suited for.

Snap also debuted a consumer-facing AI service called Specs Intelligence alongside the enterprise announcement. The assistant-style product is designed to operate across the AR glasses as well as iPhones and Mac computers. Spiegel said pricing has not been finalized, but the service will include a free tier alongside additional paid tiers based on usage.

The enterprise push comes as the broader AR and VR hardware market remains difficult. Devices including Microsoft's HoloLens and Meta's Quest-branded VR headsets have struggled to achieve mainstream commercial adoption, and the form-factor constraints of earlier hardware have been a persistent drag on the category.

Spiegel addressed the AI safety debate that has intensified across the technology industry, where foundation model developers including Anthropic and OpenAI have faced calls to slow the pace of development.

"It's really important that we coordinate an international and thoughtful response," Spiegel said. "It's really important that regulators and policymakers consider actual potential harms rather than imagined harms."

Whether Snap can convert its established consumer audience into a viable foundation for enterprise sales remains an open question. The company's core business has been built on user engagement in social features — a profile that differs substantially from the enterprise software procurement cycles it will now be navigating alongside incumbents like Salesforce and AWS.
