President Donald Trump said Friday that neither he nor SpaceX chief Elon Musk was responsible for a Starlink service outage that affected Ukraine earlier in the week, pushing back against suggestions of deliberate interference as tensions with Kyiv sharpened over renewed strikes on Russian oil infrastructure.

"I didn't do that. No, we didn't do that at all. In fact, I don't even think it was..." Trump told reporters, according to a White House pool report, stopping short of completing his explanation.

The denial came as Trump and Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky clashed publicly over Ukraine's decision to resume strikes on Russian oil refineries — a point of friction that has complicated U.S.-Ukraine relations in recent weeks.

Starlink, the satellite internet service operated by SpaceX, has been a critical communications backbone for Ukrainian military and civilian operations since Russia's full-scale invasion. Any disruption to the service draws immediate scrutiny given the network's strategic role on the battlefield.

Trump did not offer an alternative explanation for what caused the outage, nor did the White House issue a formal statement attributing the disruption to a specific technical or operational cause.

The episode also unfolded against a backdrop of broader personnel and diplomatic turbulence inside the administration. Katie Zacharia, who had served a brief and scrutiny-marked tenure as White House press secretary, was departing the post after Trump told reporters he had advised her to "reconsider" the role, a White House official told The Hill. Zacharia faced questions about her legal credentials during what officials described as a rocky start to the job.

Meanwhile, in New York City, Mayor Zohran Mamdani and other state Democratic leaders called on private parking lot owners to refuse entry to Immigration and Customs Enforcement officers — a move that added to a running series of local government pushbacks against federal immigration enforcement actions.

The convergence of the Starlink controversy, a White House staffing shake-up, and escalating domestic political clashes underscores the degree to which the administration is managing simultaneous pressure points across foreign and domestic policy fronts.

For now, the cause of the Ukraine Starlink outage remains publicly unattributed, and SpaceX has not issued a statement addressing the disruption — leaving open questions about whether the service interruption was the result of technical failure, policy decisions, or other factors that neither Trump nor Musk has explained.
