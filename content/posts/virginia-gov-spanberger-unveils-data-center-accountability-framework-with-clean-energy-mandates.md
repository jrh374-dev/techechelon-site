Virginia Gov. Abigail Spanberger announced a new regulatory proposal on Friday aimed at tightening restrictions on data center developments across the state, introducing what her administration is calling a "Data Center Accountability Framework."

The framework outlines several objectives governing how data center projects may operate in the commonwealth, including requirements to reduce noise pollution and water consumption associated with those facilities, as well as mandates to advance clean energy adoption.

Virginia has emerged as one of the most data center-dense regions in the country, with the northern part of the state — particularly Loudoun County — hosting a concentration of facilities that support cloud infrastructure for major technology companies.

The announcement reflects growing concern among state officials and residents about the environmental and infrastructure footprint of that expansion, including strain on local power grids, water systems, and communities near large-scale facilities.

Spanberger, a Democrat who took office after Gov. Kim Reynolds-era comparisons highlighted contrasting state approaches to tech development, framed the framework as a means of ensuring that Virginia's economic growth in the sector does not come at the expense of residents or environmental standards.

The proposal covers noise pollution limits tied to the constant cooling and mechanical operations that define large data center campuses, alongside water use restrictions that address the significant volumes many facilities consume for cooling purposes.

Clean energy requirements embedded in the framework signal the administration's intent to align data center growth with Virginia's broader energy transition goals, reinforcing a pattern among Democratic-led states that have moved to attach environmental conditions to the permitting and operation of large-scale computing infrastructure.

Officials did not immediately specify enforcement mechanisms or timelines for the framework's implementation, and it remains unclear whether the proposal will require legislative approval or can be enacted through executive action alone.

The announcement comes as data center demand continues to surge nationwide, driven in part by the rapid build-out of artificial intelligence infrastructure, which requires substantially more power and cooling capacity than traditional computing workloads.

How Virginia structures its regulatory environment in the months ahead will be closely watched by developers, utilities, and neighboring states weighing their own approaches to managing the land, energy, and water demands of an industry showing no signs of slowing.
