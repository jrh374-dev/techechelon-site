Palo Alto Networks reported fiscal third-quarter 2026 revenue of $3 billion, a 31% year-over-year increase that exceeded Wall Street's consensus estimate of $2.94 billion, according to LSEG, reinforcing the case that advanced AI models are expanding rather than eroding demand for enterprise security platforms.

Adjusted earnings per share rose 6% to 85 cents, topping the LSEG consensus estimate of 80 cents. Shares were roughly flat but volatile in after-hours trading following what the company described as a beat-and-raise quarter. The stock had already climbed approximately 61% for the year and 85% since the end of March.

Earlier in the year, Palo Alto's shares had come under pressure amid concern that large language models developed by companies such as Anthropic could commoditize cybersecurity services and undercut established vendors. Those fears have since receded.

The shift in sentiment accelerated after Anthropic and several major partners launched Project Glasswing in early April — an initiative designed to address heightened risks tied to the company's most advanced frontier model, Claude Mythos. Management cited the emergence of models like Mythos as a catalyst rather than a competitive threat.

"We have entered the era of truly cyber capable systems, where models like Mythos possess the autonomous capability to execute comprehensive attack campaigns from start to finish. This represents a fundamental paradigm shift for the cybersecurity industry," CEO Nikesh Arora said on the earnings call.

Arora added that rapid advancements in AI may have "increased the terminal value of the entire cybersecurity industry." He also told Jim Cramer on CNBC's "Mad Money" that the company held 1,200 customer meetings all of last year, compared with more than 800 in just the last six weeks — meetings centered on helping clients navigate cybersecurity strategy in a post-Mythos environment.

Much of that customer interest is flowing toward Palo Alto's Cortex and Agentic Endpoint Security Platforms, the company said.

Palo Alto's acquisition strategy also drew scrutiny during the quarter. Management said its $25 billion purchase of identity-security firm CyberArk — announced in late July and positioned to help secure autonomously operating AI agents — is running three to six months ahead of plan on synergy targets. CyberArk's annual recurring revenue is up 27% year over year.

Chronosphere, a smaller observability-market acquisition announced in November and completed in January, also drew positive commentary from management, which said two of the top five frontier AI labs are using the product. A deal valued at more than $200 million in ARR with a leading frontier AI lab for observability was cited as one of the quarter's key wins, alongside an $80 million deal with a separate client.

Total remaining performance obligation — business signed but not yet converted to revenue — grew 36% year over year, or 22% excluding the CyberArk and Chronosphere deals. Next-generation security annual recurring revenue climbed 60% year over year, or 28% on the same organic basis.

The company's "platformization" strategy, which consolidates multiple security functions under a single vendor relationship, added approximately 110 net new platform deals in the quarter — including 20 from identity and observability. Total platform deals reached roughly 1,650, with an additional 630 attributed to the two acquisitions. Management expressed confidence in surpassing 4,000 platform deals and $20 billion in next-generation ARR by fiscal year 2030.

The company also reiterated its target of reaching a 40% free cash flow margin in fiscal year 2028, underpinned in part by the CyberArk integration timeline.

With earnings now clearing the bar set by months of elevated expectations, attention will turn to whether Palo Alto can sustain its organic bookings acceleration and close the growing pipeline of AI-driven security engagements — a dynamic that analysts will watch closely heading into the company's fiscal fourth quarter.
