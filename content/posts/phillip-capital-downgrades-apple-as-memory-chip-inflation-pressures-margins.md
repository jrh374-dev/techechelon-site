Phillip Capital downgraded Apple to "reduce" from "neutral" on Monday, citing rising memory chip prices as a mounting threat to the iPhone maker's margins, even as demand for its hardware lineup remains steady.

Analyst Helena Wang maintained a $290 price target on Apple shares — implying a 4.4% decline from Monday's close — and warned that the company's ability to absorb higher input costs is fading.

"Memory inflation is becoming a major margin headwind. Management expects memory prices to continue increasing in 4Q26 and beyond. AAPL has partially offset the impact through lower-cost carry-in inventory, favourable product mix and lower non-memory component costs, yet these benefits are expected to diminish over time. DRAM market is effectively dominated by just three suppliers, giving AAPL little sourcing flexibility," Wang wrote in a note to clients.

Apple fell nearly 10% last Thursday after reporting quarterly earnings that beat analyst estimates on both revenue and profit but delivered weaker-than-expected guidance for the current period, citing the global chip crunch.

The company has already raised prices on its iPad and Mac models, and some analysts expect iPhones to face similar increases in the near term.

Beyond component costs, Wang flagged that Apple Intelligence — the company's suite of on-device AI features — has not yet given consumers a compelling reason to upgrade their devices.

She also pointed to regulatory obstacles in Europe and China, which together account for 44% of Apple's revenue, as factors that "could continue to limit the near-term monetisation of Apple Intelligence."

The downgrade lands as Apple navigates simultaneous pressure from a separate front: a cybersecurity breach at Tata Electronics, an Indian contract manufacturer that produces iPhones. Tata confirmed a "cybersecurity incident" to reporters but said it "had no impact on our operations across businesses," declining to specify whether any materials or data were taken.

Beijing has sought to exploit the episode as evidence of fragility in India's manufacturing capabilities — with the Communist Party's Global Times warning in July of "systemic weaknesses in 'Made in India'" — as China works to retain its position as the dominant hub for Apple's supply chain.

Despite Monday's downgrade, broader Wall Street sentiment on Apple remains constructive. According to LSEG data, 32 analysts rate the stock as buy or strong buy, 13 rate it as hold, and only 3 assign an underperform rating.

How durably memory prices weigh on Apple's gross margins — and whether the company can pass those costs to consumers through iPhone price increases without dampening demand — will likely be the central question heading into its fourth-quarter guidance cycle.
