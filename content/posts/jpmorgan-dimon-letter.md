JPMorgan Chase CEO Jamie Dimon released his annual letter to shareholders Thursday morning, and for the first time in five years he devoted substantial space — four pages — to private credit. The framing was unusual for him.

"The mechanics of private credit," Dimon wrote, "now resemble the pre-2007 securitization market more than is comfortable. Risk is being repriced privately, recorded illiquidly, and stress-tested against assumptions that have not been tested at scale."

The letter is annually parsed for shifts in tone. This year's was unmistakable. Dimon used the word "fragility" eleven times, "concentrated" seven, and "discipline" four. The previous letter used those words a combined twelve times.

## What he's worried about

Dimon's substantive concern is that the private credit market has scaled past $1.7 trillion without ever experiencing a default cycle. The assumptions that underwrite individual deals — recovery rates, refinancing windows, sponsor backstops — have been calibrated in a benign environment. When the cycle turns, those assumptions will be tested simultaneously, across many funds, in instruments that mark to model rather than to market.

That is the structural setup he says he has not seen since 2006.
