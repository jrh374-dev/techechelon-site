Bengaluru-based vibe-coding startup Emergent AI raised $300 million in a Series C funding round on Wednesday, achieving a valuation of $1.5 billion and becoming the second Indian artificial intelligence company to cross the unicorn threshold within a single month.

Creaegis, a Bengaluru-based investment firm, led the round and was joined by India family office firm Claypond and California-based Sentinel Global. Existing backers Khosla Ventures, SoftBank Vision Fund 2, Lightspeed, and Y Combinator also participated.

Emergent was founded one year ago and positions itself as a tool for non-technical users. The company said approximately 12 million applications were built on its platform in the past year by small business owners and solo entrepreneurs.

"We built Emergent for the non-technical entrepreneur and the small business owner, with 70% of our users having no prior coding experience," said Mukund Jha, co-founder and chief executive of Emergent.

The fundraise follows Sarvam, India's full-stack sovereign AI company, raising $234 billion from a group of investors roughly a month ago and reaching a post-money valuation of $1.5 billion. The two milestones, arriving in close succession, have drawn attention to India's efforts to establish itself as a meaningful player in the global AI landscape.

Analysts say the developments reflect genuine momentum, even if they stop well short of signaling a structural shift. Deepika Giri, head of research for AI, analytics, and data at IDC Asia Pacific, said the back-to-back unicorn valuations are "consistent with broader momentum we're tracking," adding that "nearly half of Indian enterprises are already testing agentic AI solutions." She described this as an "unusually fast experimentation and workforce automation at scale for a market this size."

Market research firm IDC expects 45% of Indian organizations to use specialized cloud services by 2026 to gain computing access, which analysts say could ease a key bottleneck for AI training and inference. IDC also notes that India has the broadest AI accelerator stack in the Asia-Pacific region — spanning Nvidia, AMD, and hyperscaler silicon — giving it a hardware flexibility that most regional markets lack.

Mohammad Hassan, head of APAC dividend forecasting at S&P Global Market Intelligence, said Sarvam's funding reflects confidence that India can build "valuable intellectual property in indigenous and multilingual AI." Emergent's fundraise, he added, highlights the strength of Indian technology talent, which could prove to be "India's trump card in the long run AI race" — though he characterized both developments as "positive signals" rather than proof of a major shift.

India's position in the global AI race still carries significant structural limitations. The country does not yet produce cutting-edge chips domestically, lacks a frontier-scale foundation model comparable to leading U.S. or Chinese systems, and trails the AI leaders considerably in data center capacity.

Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi articulated a broader ambition at a global AI summit in February, stating his vision was for India to be "among the top three AI superpowers globally," not merely as a consumer but also as a creator. The country's substantial IT services export base gives it a large pool of software talent capable of building AI applications, though analysts note that securing unfettered access to foreign foundational models remains a key strategic risk.

Neil Shah, vice president of research at Counterpoint Research, said it will take "at least three to four years" for India's AI ecosystem to grow to a point where it creates a "flywheel effect," suggesting the back-to-back unicorn announcements are an early marker on a longer road rather than the destination itself.
