Mistral AI, the Paris-based startup positioning itself as Europe's leading artificial intelligence company, raised 3 billion euros ($3.5 billion) in fresh funding led by South Korean memory chip giant Samsung Electronics, the company said Tuesday, giving it a post-money valuation of more than 21 billion euros.

The round also included the Scaleup Europe Fund — an EU-backed vehicle managed by EQT whose investors include the European Commission, Novo Holdings, and Santander — as well as existing investor PSG Equity.

The new valuation nearly doubles Mistral's previous mark of 11.7 billion euros, which was set roughly a year ago following a funding round led by Dutch chip equipment manufacturer ASML.

Arthur Mensch, Mistral's chief executive, told CNBC in an interview that the proceeds would go toward building out the company's own data center infrastructure and renting additional computing capacity in the interim. "Long term, the plan is to fully rely on capacity that we are building ourselves, and so that means that the amount of compute that we own is going to grow ... around 100% in the next five years," Mensch said.

Mensch added that the company plans to train "bigger and faster models" on that expanded infrastructure.

Mistral's business model centers on working directly with large enterprises to build custom AI tools tailored to their workflows — a strategy that sets it apart from OpenAI and Anthropic, which largely offer general-purpose, closed-source systems. The company already integrates its AI into the manufacturing processes of ASML, and Mensch said Samsung would become a focus for a similar partnership.

On the revenue front, Mensch said earlier this year that he expected Mistral's annual recurring revenue to exceed $1 billion in 2026. With the new round in place, he said he expects "to be beating" that figure "if everything happens as they are trending," though he declined to offer a revised number.

"We're very confident that the fundraising we are doing today is also accelerating and enabling further growth down the line in 2027," Mensch said.

Mistral has built much of its market identity around open-weight AI models, pitching itself as a transparent, European-controlled alternative in a landscape dominated by American and Chinese players. That positioning has gained traction with governments and corporations seeking what the industry calls "sovereign AI" — the ability to run powerful models on their own terms, free from dependence on foreign platforms.

Mensch acknowledged the competitive pressure from Chinese AI labs but argued their enterprise reach outside China remains limited. He said European companies face uncertainty about whether Chinese models will be upgraded consistently over time or become subject to export restrictions. "At this point in time, we are seeing that the volatility in this space is actually quite extreme," Mensch said.

He framed Mistral's ability to guarantee continuous model improvements as a key differentiator. "The only way we can provide that guarantee is by continuing to train our models ourselves," he said.

With $2 billion-plus rounds now becoming a benchmark for top-tier AI infrastructure buildouts, Mistral's financing signals that European AI challengers are increasingly able to attract the kind of capital that was once concentrated almost exclusively in Silicon Valley.
