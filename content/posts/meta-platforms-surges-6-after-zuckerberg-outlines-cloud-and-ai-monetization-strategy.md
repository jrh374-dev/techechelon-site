Meta Platforms closed the week ending July 11 with a 6% single-session gain on Friday, as CEO Mark Zuckerberg offered investors the clearest picture yet of how the company intends to convert its vast artificial intelligence spending into revenue.

The rally capped a week in which Meta methodically laid out multiple new commercial initiatives, addressing what analysts had identified as the central question hanging over its stock: the return on its AI infrastructure investments.

The week's groundwork had been laid on July 1, when Meta confirmed it is preparing to launch a cloud business that would sell excess computing capacity to outside customers. The move would place Meta in direct competition with Amazon Web Services, Microsoft Azure, and Alphabet's Google Cloud, as well as smaller providers such as CoreWeave and SpaceX.

On Tuesday, Meta launched Muse Image, an AI image-generation model targeting creators and advertisers. The technology is set to power new tools within Meta's Advantage Plus advertising platform, which allows brands to develop ad creative and automate certain marketing tasks.

Then on Thursday, the company unveiled Muse Spark 1.1, which it described as "its strongest model yet for coding and agentic AI tasks." Meta said it would charge developers to access the technology — a notable departure from the company's prior emphasis on open-source, freely distributed AI models. The move puts Meta in more direct competition with OpenAI's Codex and Anthropic's Claude Code.

Zuckerberg's comments in an interview with Bloomberg News contributed to Friday's share-price move. "The offers that you get for using the compute are so high that it may make sense, in some cases, to rent out or consider those kind of deals instead of your own internal uses," he told Bloomberg.

On the infrastructure side, Meta is also moving to reduce its dependence on outside chip suppliers. The company plans to begin manufacturing its custom AI chip in September, according to a report Thursday, as it looks to double its computing capacity in the coming year. The chip is co-designed with Broadcom and will be manufactured by Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co., a supply arrangement that could help lower Meta's computing costs and reduce its reliance on Nvidia and AMD.

Meta's week unfolded against a broader backdrop of semiconductor volatility. The VanEck Semiconductor ETF swung sharply throughout the week — rising roughly 2% on Monday, dropping nearly 4% on Tuesday after Samsung's quarterly results disappointed investors and reports emerged that China's DeepSeek is developing its own AI chip, then recovering 2.5% on Thursday.

One stabilizing force mid-week was Apple's announcement of an expanded multiyear partnership with Broadcom, expected to exceed $30 billion, covering more than 15 billion U.S.-made chips and including a $1.5 billion expansion of Broadcom's facility in Fort Collins, Colorado. Broadcom shares climbed nearly 5% on Wednesday and finished the week up 10%.

Nvidia rose 4% on Friday, closing at $210.96 per share — its highest close in nearly a month.

At the index level, the Nasdaq gained 1.74% for the week and the S&P 500 rose 1.23%, even as the Dow Jones Industrial Average ended down 0.5% after renewed U.S.-Iran tensions erased an early-week record close above 53,000.

The week's developments signal that Meta is shifting from AI infrastructure build-out to active commercialization across multiple fronts simultaneously — cloud services, developer tools, and proprietary silicon — underscoring how quickly the competitive dynamics among large-scale AI platforms are evolving heading into the second half of 2026.
