Four of the largest technology companies in the United States — Amazon, Alphabet, Meta, and Microsoft — now rank among the country's top capital investors, driven by their accelerating spending on artificial intelligence infrastructure, according to new research from the Progressive Policy Institute.

The report, published by the Washington-based think tank on Wednesday, found that the four firms combined to invest more than half of a benchmark total tracked in the study, underscoring how concentrated AI-related capital expenditure has become among a small cluster of platform companies.

The findings reflect a broader shift in the composition of U.S. corporate investment, one in which technology firms — historically associated with asset-light business models — are now committing capital at a scale that rivals traditional heavy industries such as manufacturing, energy, and telecommunications.

AI infrastructure spending has surged across the sector as companies race to build and operate the data centers, custom chips, and networking capacity required to train and deploy large-scale models. That buildout has driven sustained increases in capital expenditure guidance from each of the four companies named in the PPI report.

The report's release comes as Wall Street continues to scrutinize the return profile of that spending. Bank of America reiterated buy ratings on Nvidia, Marvell, Broadcom, and Micron on Wednesday, describing several semiconductor stocks as compelling given their exposure to AI infrastructure demand. "Overall, we maintain our bullish stance on key AI infra semis beneficiaries, with top sector picks: NVDA, MRVL, AMD, AVGO, MU, LRCX," the firm said in a note.

Morgan Stanley, meanwhile, reiterated Microsoft as overweight, citing the company's recent dividend increase and earnings trajectory. "Microsoft increased its quarterly dividend by 7 cents or 8% to $0.98 per share," the firm said, adding that combined with "high-teens EPS growth, this supports a durable high-teens total return profile at MSFT, framing an attractive risk/reward."

Citi also reiterated a buy rating on Meta ahead of the company's Connect conference scheduled for September 23 and 24, adding a positive catalyst watch on shares. "Ahead of Meta Connect on 9/23-24 whereby we expect Meta to update its AI product strategy, we are adding an upside 90-day Catalyst Watch on shares of META," Citi said.

The PPI report arrives amid a broader policy debate over the societal implications of the AI buildout — including its energy consumption, labor market effects, and the concentration of compute resources among a handful of companies.

The Progressive Policy Institute, which describes itself as a center-left think tank, has generally viewed large-scale technology investment favorably as a driver of productivity and economic growth, though the full report is expected to address the distributional dimensions of that spending.

For investors and policymakers alike, the ranking signals that AI capital expenditure is no longer a rounding error in the U.S. investment landscape — it is now a primary driver of aggregate corporate spending, with the four named companies functioning less like traditional software businesses and more like capital-intensive infrastructure operators.
