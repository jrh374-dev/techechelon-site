A U.S.-based startup has filed suit against Apple in India, alleging that the iPhone maker's NameDrop contact-sharing feature infringes a patented technology the company developed years before Apple introduced the capability.

GG Technologies, the Santa Monica-headquartered firm behind contact-exchange app StayTouch, is seeking ₹15 billion — roughly $161.86 million — in damages along with an injunction that would prohibit Apple from selling or importing iPhones and Apple Watches preloaded with NameDrop in India, according to court documents [first reported by TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/20/startup-sues-apple-in-india-over-tap-to-share-contacts-feature).

The company filed its 781-page complaint with the Bombay High Court in October, following the collapse of mediation proceedings that had begun in April and concluded without resolution in September. Pre-institution mediation is generally required before filing commercial lawsuits in India.

GG Technologies argues that NameDrop — which allows users to share contact information by holding two Apple devices close together — replicates the core functionality covered by its Indian patent on proximity-based contact exchange without manual pairing.

The startup launched StayTouch in 2019, the same year it first sought patent protection in the United States. It filed its Indian patent application in 2021 and received that patent in July 2024. Apple introduced NameDrop as part of iOS 17 in June 2023, with the feature debuting on iPhones later that year and arriving on compatible Apple Watches in October via watchOS 10.1.

"We are seeking an injunction to restrict the usage of the NameDrop feature which is native to iOS," GG Technologies told TechCrunch.

The India case runs parallel to a separate legal effort in the United States. GG Technologies sued Apple in the U.S. in April, and the International Trade Commission opened a formal investigation into Apple's alleged patent infringement in August following the startup's complaint. That proceeding could result in a ban on imports of infringing devices into the country.

The case in India has yet to be listed for a hearing.

GG Technologies was founded in 2018 by Gaurav Goel, a graduate of the Indian Institute of Technology Delhi and a former Goldman Sachs investment banker, who built StayTouch as a digital alternative to business cards. The app is available on both iOS and Android.

Apple did not respond to a request for comment.

The lawsuit arrives as India holds growing strategic importance for Apple — both as a consumer market where iPhones account for less than 10% of smartphone shipments, leaving considerable room for expansion, and as a manufacturing base the company has been developing as an alternative to China. An injunction, if granted, could complicate Apple's operations in one of its most consequential growth markets, underscoring the stakes of litigation that began as a small-startup dispute and has since expanded to two jurisdictions.
