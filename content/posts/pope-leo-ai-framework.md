Pope Leo XIV delivered his first major technology address on Wednesday from the Apostolic Palace, calling for an "enforceable international framework" on artificial intelligence deployment and inviting the chief executives of OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, and Mistral to a closed-door convening at the Vatican on October 14.

The address ran 47 minutes. It was substantively the most specific commentary on AI deployment any sitting pontiff has delivered. Leo's predecessors had urged caution in general terms; Leo named specific risks — labor displacement, autonomous weapons, model concentration — and proposed specific responses, including an international body modeled on the IAEA.

## Why this lands differently

The Vatican is now the rare global actor with no financial stake in the outcome of AI policy. Brussels and Beijing have both been positioning around competitive advantage; Washington is split between safety-focused officials and capital-allocation ones. Leo's intervention reorients the conversation around a moral framework that few governments can credibly object to without appearing parochial.

EU Commissioner for AI policy Pia Berthou, in a statement issued two hours after the address, said the bloc "welcomed His Holiness's leadership." White House response was more measured: a spokesperson called the address "a thoughtful contribution to an ongoing global discussion."
