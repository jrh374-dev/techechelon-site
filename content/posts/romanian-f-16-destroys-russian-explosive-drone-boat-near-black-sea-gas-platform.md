Romania's military destroyed a Russian explosive-laden drone boat found within a few hundred meters of the Neptun Deep offshore gas project in the Black Sea on August 20, marking the second suspected Russian drone incident near the site this month.

The Romanian Coast Guard first spotted the maritime drone near the platform's main drilling rig, where several hundred workers were engaged in the facility's ongoing installation, Defense Minister Radu Miruță said in a Facebook post.

The Romanian military scrambled two F-16 fighter jets, one of which used its cannons to disable the surface drone. Army specialists then carried out a controlled detonation, footage shared by the defense minister showed.

Romania's president blamed Russia directly for the incident. "I strongly condemn the intensification of these types of irresponsible incidents on the part of the Russian Federation," wrote Nicușor Dan on X. "We remain vigilant alongside NATO allies to defend ourselves and repel such challenges."

The Neptun Deep project is a significant energy asset for the European Union. Once it reaches full production in 2027, it would double Romania's gas output and help supply countries including Germany and Moldova, reinforcing its strategic importance as a potential target.

This was the second drone-related incident near the site in August alone. On August 11, the Romanian military destroyed two Russian-made flying drones found floating near Neptun Deep. In both cases, Romania confirmed with Ukrainian partners that the drones were not Ukrainian in origin.

H.I. Sutton, an independent naval analyst who runs the website Covert Shores, described the drone boat as an unknown type distinct from surface drones Russia has previously deployed. Sutton identified features including a "large hinged hangar in the forward hull" that may have been designed to carry missiles or aerial drones, as well as two flat antennas potentially enabling satellite communications via services such as Starlink, alongside radar and other sensors.

The incident, [first reported by Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/gadgets/2026/08/explosive-russian-drone-boat-destroyed-near-european-offshore-gas-site), reflects a broader pattern of escalating drone activity across the Black Sea region since Russia launched its full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022.

Ukraine has used flying drones, missiles, and drone boats to damage Russian warships and disrupt energy and grain shipping. Russia, in turn, has struck Ukrainian ports and grain export infrastructure, with analysts warning that such constraints could threaten global food supplies if alternative export routes are not secured.

European airspace has also seen spillover. On August 16, two Spanish F-18 fighter jets deployed in Romania for a NATO mission intercepted and destroyed a drone that a NATO spokesperson described as being of Russian origin.

Separately, U.S. intelligence experts have described Russia as likely responsible for an attempted drone attack on Ukrainian cargo aircraft parked at Leipzig airport in Germany, though the German government has yet to formally attribute responsibility.

With Neptun Deep on track to make Romania the European Union's largest gas producer, the platform's vulnerability to drone incursions is expected to remain a focal point for both NATO planners and European energy policymakers as the conflict in Ukraine continues to reshape security calculations across the region.
