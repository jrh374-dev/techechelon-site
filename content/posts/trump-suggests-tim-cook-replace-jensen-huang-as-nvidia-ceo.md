President Donald Trump floated Apple's executive chairman Tim Cook as a potential successor to Jensen Huang at Nvidia, following the chip giant's CEO resignation, according to a post Trump made on Truth Social Thursday.

"Only one man" could take Huang's place, Trump wrote, identifying Cook by name. "A Legend replaces a Legend!" he added.

Cook stepped down as Apple's chief executive on September 1, 2026, after 15 years leading the company. John Ternus assumed the CEO role, while Cook transitioned to executive chairman of Apple's board of directors — a position that keeps him engaged with global policymakers and international relationships, including Apple's dealings with China.

Trump was not the only figure to put Cook's name forward. Jeffrey Sonnenfeld, Senior Associate Dean for Leadership Studies at Yale School of Management, published an op-ed in Time on Wednesday arguing that Cook deserved serious consideration for the role. Sonnenfeld cited Cook's "unparalleled track record of succeeding a bigger-than-life founder," drawing a parallel to Cook's transition from Apple co-founder Steve Jobs.

Whether Trump's remarks reflect a considered opinion or an offhand observation is unclear. The White House did not elaborate on the post.

Huang's departure — [first reported by TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/12/trump-floats-apple-chairman-tim-cook-as-jensen-huangs-successor) — has set off a wide-ranging conversation among corporate governance experts and industry observers about leadership continuity at one of the most consequential companies in the artificial intelligence supply chain.

Nvidia has been central to the buildout of AI infrastructure globally, with its chips powering data centers operated by major cloud providers and AI developers. The question of who leads the company next carries significant weight for technology supply chains, government procurement, and investor sentiment alike.

Cook, for his part, has not publicly commented on the speculation. His current role at Apple focuses on board oversight and international diplomacy rather than day-to-day operations, a narrower scope than the demands of a Nvidia CEO position would likely require.

Sonnenfeld's op-ed framed the question as one of executive succession competence, arguing that navigating a founder-centric culture — as Cook did following Steve Jobs's death in 2011 — is a rare and transferable skill.

The parallel has limits. Cook inherited a consumer electronics and software ecosystem; Nvidia operates at the intersection of semiconductor manufacturing, enterprise computing, and national security contracting, each with its own set of stakeholder demands.

The search for Huang's successor is expected to draw scrutiny from investors, U.S. government officials, and international partners, underscoring how thoroughly Nvidia's leadership has become intertwined with the broader trajectory of artificial intelligence development.
