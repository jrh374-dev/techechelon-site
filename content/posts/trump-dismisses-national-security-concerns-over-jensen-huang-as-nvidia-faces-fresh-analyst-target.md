President Donald Trump said Monday he would welcome former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang's return to the company he co-founded, downplaying national security concerns stemming from Huang's extramarital relationship with an alleged Chinese spy as Wall Street analysts continued to lower their price targets on the chipmaker ahead of its earnings report.

"I think he learned a big lesson," Trump told reporters outside the White House before departing for a trip to Asia. Asked whether U.S. national security had been compromised, the president said, "It was not put at risk, and our country is now stronger than ever before."

Huang resigned from Nvidia last Thursday following reports of multiple extramarital affairs, including one with Alice Huang, a former executive at Megaspeed — a Singapore-based technology company that U.S. officials suspect of having funneled Nvidia's advanced chips to China. Nvidia is the leading global supplier of advanced artificial intelligence chips.

The scandal has drawn scrutiny from both Republican and Democratic members of Congress. A spokesperson for House Foreign Affairs Committee Chair Rep. Brian Mast told reporters Monday that "negotiations are still going" regarding Jensen Huang's potential testimony before a trio of House committees. Mast, along with House Select China Committee Chair Rep. John Moolenaar and House Intelligence Committee Chair Rep. Rick Crawford, had given Huang an 8 p.m. ET Friday deadline to respond to their request to appear. Mast has said he would subpoena the former CEO if he declines voluntarily.

Trump also defended Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick, whom critics have accused of impeding a crackdown on companies suspected of diverting Nvidia's advanced AI chips to China. Axios reported last week that Lutnick had "repeatedly stood in the way" of such enforcement actions, prompting a Situation Room confrontation with Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent. Trump acknowledged Monday that Lutnick may have delayed adding Megaspeed to the Commerce Department's entity list, but framed the delay as a diplomatic calculation. "He was doing that because you could have — if you didn't delay it, if you didn't do something — you could have a blow up with China," Trump said.

On the analyst side, Mizuho lowered its price target on Nvidia shares to $220 from $250, while maintaining an outperform rating. The firm's analyst Vijay Rakesh said in a note to clients that the stock's roughly 32% decline from its October peak — compared with a 23% drop in the SOX semiconductor index — reflects what he called the "Jensen/founder premium lost." Rakesh also cited a "6-7-month management overhang" as the company comes under government scrutiny.

UBS, which carries a buy rating on the stock, cut its target to $240 from $300. Analyst Timothy Arcuri said in a Monday note that concerns about Nvidia's longer-term product roadmap are "overblown," arguing the ecosystem would continue to drive the company's annual product cadence regardless of who leads it. He did note, however, that "the new more dense Kyber racks are now off the table."

Nvidia shares were last trading approximately 33% below their record high of $243.37, reached on October 6. The stock has fallen roughly 9% over the past five trading days.

Arcuri said he expects Nvidia to post strong figures in its upcoming earnings report, which is scheduled for Tuesday after the market close — a result he said could help stabilize shares.

Trump said he may raise the Jensen Huang matter with Chinese President Xi Jinping at the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit, reversing an earlier statement in which he said he had no plans to do so. Both Lutnick and Bessent are traveling with the president to Asia, as is Secretary of State Marco Rubio, who reportedly warned Trump about Huang's relationship with Alice Huang as early as May.
