Nvidia has launched an internal investigation into its chief executive, Jensen Huang, after a leaked government memo linked him to a woman allegedly recruited by China's Ministry of State Security, raising questions about potential exposure of sensitive corporate information and the integrity of U.S. chip export controls.

The figure at the center of the matter is Alice Huang — no relation to Jensen — a former television reporter who became an executive at a company called Megaspeed. According to reporting by The New York Times, Megaspeed acquired $4.8 billion worth of hard-to-obtain Nvidia chips beginning around the period when Alice Huang was photographed with Jensen Huang at a Taipei bar in 2024.

Megaspeed has ties to the Chinese military and operates data centers in Southeast Asia — with offices in Singapore and Indonesia — to sell compute capacity to Alibaba. The chips the company uses for that purpose are banned from being imported directly into China.

Secretary of State Marco Rubio reportedly oversaw a memo detailing Alice Huang's alleged recruitment by the Chinese Ministry of State Security and her relationship with Jensen Huang. The memo circulated in May, ahead of President Donald Trump's trip to China. Trump was personally briefed on the findings, according to the Times, and took no action.

Jensen Huang initially was not included on the list of invitees for Trump's China trip, but was added after Trump saw media coverage of the perceived exclusion — a detail that underscores the closeness of the two figures.

Nvidia spokesperson John Rizzo told the Times that Huang's interactions with Alice Huang were "not unusual" and consistent with meetings he holds with any of Nvidia's many customers. Rizzo also said the company does not disclose classified information to customers and that Nvidia's customers do not ask the company to lobby for changes to chip restrictions.

Two days after that statement, Jensen Huang sent a company-wide email to Nvidia employees acknowledging he had been "unfaithful" and directing remarks to his wife and family. His son serves as a director of product management at Nvidia, his daughter as a senior marketing director, and his son-in-law also holds a position at the company.

The governance structure of Nvidia adds further complexity. Huang has 60 direct reports, a configuration that analysts say means any compromised judgment or instructions from the top could reach virtually every corner of the company. Nvidia said it is opening an investigation into Huang, who is also the company's largest individual shareholder.

Nvidia shares fell nearly 5 percent following the revelations. Megaspeed's business model — acquiring chips that are restricted from Chinese import — means the company has little financial incentive to lobby for the removal of those restrictions, unless, investigators may argue, profit is not the primary motivation.

Nvidia occupies a central position in the broader AI ecosystem. The company holds investments in SpaceX, CoreWeave, and OpenAI, among others, and has been an active lender of support to AI-focused cloud providers known as neoclouds. Nvidia helped stabilize CoreWeave's IPO when it was under pressure earlier this year. CoreWeave shares were up approximately 6 percent at the time of publication.

Senator Elizabeth Warren of Massachusetts has signaled she intends to scrutinize the matter, and the political environment may amplify the pressure on Nvidia. Democrats recently regained control of Congress, and several politicians across both parties are seen as positioning themselves ahead of the next presidential election cycle — with Nvidia's relationship to the Trump administration now a potential liability in that calculation.

The episode poses a broader question for the AI industry: Nvidia functions, by some analysts' descriptions, as the effective central bank of AI infrastructure. Any sustained instability at the company could ripple across the neoclouds and AI ventures that depend on its financing and chip supply. The scope of the internal investigation, and whether federal authorities launch a parallel inquiry, will likely determine how long that uncertainty persists.
