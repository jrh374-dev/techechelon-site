Nvidia has crossed $40 billion in equity commitments in 2026, cementing its position not just as the dominant supplier of artificial intelligence chips but as one of the most active investors in the AI infrastructure ecosystem.

The pace of dealmaking has been swift. This week alone, the company reached an agreement giving it the right to invest up to $2.1 billion in data center operator IREN and, a day earlier, struck a pact with Corning allowing it to invest up to $3.2 billion in the 175-year-old glass manufacturer. Shares of both companies rose following the announcements.

The deals fit a broader pattern. Nvidia has signed at least seven multibillion-dollar investments this year with publicly traded companies and participated in roughly two dozen investment rounds in private companies, including some early-stage deals, according to FactSet.

The single largest commitment was a $30 billion check for OpenAI. The company also participated in substantial funding rounds for Anthropic and Elon Musk's xAI, shortly before xAI merged with SpaceX in February.

"There are so many great, amazing foundation model companies, and we try to invest in all of them," CEO Jensen Huang said during an April podcast appearance. "We don't pick winners. We need to support everyone."

Earlier in the year, Nvidia put $2 billion into CoreWeave as part of a data center buildout agreement, and another $2 billion into Nebius Group, an AI cloud company, for AI infrastructure deployment and design work. In March, it invested $2 billion each in Marvell Technology, Lumentum, and Coherent — all companies working on photonics technologies central to next-generation networking.

The strategy reflects an effort to finance the entire AI supply chain, ensuring that the hardware running across that supply chain is Nvidia's. The IREN deal, for instance, includes an agreement for the data center company to deploy up to 5 gigawatts of Nvidia's DSX-branded infrastructure designs at facilities worldwide. The Corning agreement involves building three new U.S. facilities dedicated to optical technologies for Nvidia as it shifts toward fiber-optic cables in its rack-scale systems.

Nvidia generated $97 billion in free cash flow in its last fiscal year, providing the financial runway to sustain the investment push. Non-marketable equity securities on the company's balance sheet — private company investments — grew to $22.25 billion at the end of January, up from $3.39 billion a year earlier. Reported gains on private and public equity holdings reached $8.92 billion, compared with $1.03 billion in the prior fiscal year.

A significant driver of those gains has been Nvidia's bet on Intel, which the company valued at $5 billion at the time of investment. That position is now worth over $25 billion, reflecting Intel's stock rising well over 200% this year.

During Nvidia's last earnings call in February, Huang said, "Our investments are focused very squarely, strategically on expanding and deepening our ecosystem reach."

Not everyone views the approach as straightforwardly constructive. Matthew Bryson, an analyst at Wedbush Securities, wrote in a note that Nvidia's investments fit "squarely into the circular investment theme" that has been driving concerns about the market's durability — a dynamic in which Nvidia backs companies that buy its chips, sometimes leasing compute back to those same companies. Critics have drawn comparisons to vendor financing practices that contributed to the dot-com bubble's inflation.

Bryson, however, also said the investments could underscore Nvidia's vision and build a "competitive moat" if the company executes. Chip analyst Jordan Klein at Mizuho called the deals with component makers "super smart by the CFO and team and a great use of cash," citing the acceleration of critical technologies.

An Nvidia spokesperson did not respond to a request for comment.

With Nvidia's fiscal first-quarter earnings report less than two weeks away, investors will gain a fuller picture of the portfolio's size and its impact on the company's financials.
