Waymo announced Tuesday that it intends to launch driverless ride-hailing services in Munich, Germany, by the end of 2027, marking the Alphabet-owned robotaxi company's entry into its first European Union market.

The company said it would begin "phased" testing in Munich in the coming weeks, with the goal of adapting its autonomous driving software to the city's "distinct driving conditions" before a commercial launch.

Munich is Waymo's third international market overall. The company is already conducting vehicle tests in London and Tokyo, but neither of those cities is within the EU, making the Munich announcement a distinct regulatory and geographic milestone.

"Munich is a world-class hub for mobility and engineering, and becoming part of this city marks an important milestone in our global expansion," Waymo co-CEO Tekedra Mawakana said in a blog post on Tuesday.

The expansion comes as Waymo continues to scale aggressively in the United States. The company currently operates in 11 U.S. cities and has announced plans to extend service to 19 additional domestic markets.

Germany presents a markedly different operating environment than the United States. European regulators have developed their own frameworks for autonomous vehicle certification and road testing, and Munich's dense urban street network — with its mix of cyclists, trams, and high pedestrian volume — will require substantial software adaptation before full driverless deployment can proceed.

The announcement also arrives at a moment of heightened investor attention on autonomous vehicle technology more broadly. Waymo's parent company, Alphabet, is set to report alongside other major technology firms amid continued scrutiny of AI infrastructure spending and returns.

The company has not disclosed the scale of its Munich testing fleet, the regulatory approvals it has already secured from German authorities, or the specific corridors it plans to operate once phased testing begins. Those details are expected to emerge as the testing program ramps through the remainder of 2026.

With a commercial launch target set for late 2027, Munich will serve as a proving ground for whether Waymo's software stack — built primarily on American road data — can be retooled at pace for European urban conditions, a question that will shape the company's broader continental ambitions.
