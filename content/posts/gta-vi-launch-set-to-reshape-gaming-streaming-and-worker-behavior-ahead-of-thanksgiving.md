Grand Theft Auto VI is set to launch on Nov. 19, and analysts, employers, and media executives are bracing for an economic ripple effect that extends well beyond the video game industry.

The release comes 13 years after Rockstar Games published Grand Theft Auto V, and demand indicators suggest the new title could set records. Sensor Tower estimated more than 12 million preorders through Nov. 13, while Morgan Stanley forecast the game could sell 40 million copies by March 2027.

Roth analyst Eric Handler estimated that Rockstar's parent company, Take-Two Interactive, spent more than $1 billion developing the game — a figure that reflects two launch delays before the November release date was set.

Take-Two CEO Strauss Zelnick addressed early demand signals in a recent interview, saying, "Our expectations were high and they are being vastly exceeded so far."

Investor sentiment has tracked the anticipation. Take-Two's stock has more than doubled since the beginning of 2023, underscoring how much of the company's near-term valuation hinges on the title's performance.

The game's reach appears to extend into the labor market. BambooHR, a workplace time-off platform, reported that paid time off requests for the GTA VI release date are nearly 13% higher than equivalent days in 2024 and 2025, with nearly one in five workers planning to take leave around the launch.

PwC issued a report examining sectors likely to benefit from — or be hurt by — the release. The firm projected a surge in food delivery orders from homebound gamers and flagged potential tourism benefits for Miami, which features prominently in the game's setting.

Bart Spiegel, who leads PwC's Global Entertainment and Media sector, said the timing creates direct competition with traditional Thanksgiving media consumption. "How are people spending Thanksgiving? There's a lot of sports on TV, but you've also got a pretty solid group of your demographics that are going to be focused on playing 'GTA VI,'" Spiegel said. "You're going to start to pull eyeballs away from video streaming platforms."

Spiegel added that the downstream effects on streaming metrics could surface in corporate earnings calls. "I think you're going to hear a lot of analyst calls in the first quarter of 2027 saying, 'Hey, listen, some of our numbers were down because of the release of this game.'"

Music streaming has already seen a preview of the game's cultural pull. Tom Petty's "Love Is a Long Road," which appeared in the first GTA VI trailer in December 2023, reportedly saw Spotify streams jump by more than 37,000%.

Streaming content creators are positioning the launch as a growth catalyst. Twitch Chief Product Officer Mike Minton described the game as a sustained opportunity rather than a one-time event. "It goes beyond this launch moment into this sustaining opportunity for them to grow, build, engage and retain their audience and ultimately support themselves by making money," Minton said.

A creator known as Deansocool, who has 375,000 followers on Twitch, was more direct in his assessment. "This game is going to make a lot of millionaires in 2027," he said in an interview. "A lot of creators gotta understand this is it. This is that golden ticket. This is like a hundred times bigger than Fortnite."

GTA V's durability offers a reference point for how long such engagement can last — the 2013 title remains one of the top five most viewed games on Twitch in 2026.

Rockstar Games declined an interview request. The studio has not publicly commented on sales expectations or its broader content strategy ahead of the Nov. 19 launch.
