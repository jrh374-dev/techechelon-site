Apple's MacBook Air is now facing stock constraints driven by the same global memory chip shortage that has already disrupted supply of the Mac mini and Mac Studio, underscoring how AI-driven demand for semiconductors is rippling across the consumer electronics market.

The shortage, [first reported by TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/02/the-global-memory-shortage-hits-the-macbook-air) citing Bloomberg's Mark Gurman, stems from chip-hungry AI companies consuming memory supply at a pace that is squeezing availability for traditional consumer hardware.

Customers attempting to purchase a MacBook Air directly from Apple's website are currently being told to expect delivery in the back half of August. Certain configurations are pushing into September, retailers say.

Apple is responding on two fronts, according to Gurman. The company is raising prices and working to source memory from Chinese suppliers to ease the supply crunch.

The shortage has also reshaped Apple's marketing approach. The company delayed its annual back-to-school promotion from its typical June launch window. In what appears to be a departure from past campaigns, the current marketing materials center on the base model of the MacBook Pro rather than the MacBook Air — and include a disclosure that reads, "MacBook Air subject to availability."

The MacBook Air is Apple's most popular Mac, making the supply constraint particularly significant. The Mac mini and Mac Studio, which faced similar shortages earlier, occupy narrower segments of the Mac lineup. The Air's position as Apple's entry-level, high-volume laptop means any sustained shortage could weigh meaningfully on near-term Mac sales.

The broader context is a memory market under strain from infrastructure buildout by large technology companies. Amazon CEO Andy Jassy said on his company's most recent earnings call that even at a 2026 capital expenditure budget of $220 billion, Amazon would not have enough capacity to meet all demand that year — a dynamic he expects to persist into 2027. Jassy also noted that demand already booked for 2028 is, in his word, "striking."

That level of spending, mirrored by Alphabet, Microsoft, and Meta Platforms, has intensified competition for the memory and compute components that also go into consumer devices like the MacBook Air.

Apple has not publicly commented on a timeline for normalizing MacBook Air inventory. How quickly the company can secure alternative memory supply — and whether price increases absorb or suppress consumer demand — will determine how long the shortage affects its flagship laptop line.
