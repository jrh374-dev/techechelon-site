The capital will also fund Shield AI's acquisition of Aechelon Technology, a maker of flight simulation software used to train U.S. military pilots. Financial terms of the acquisition were not disclosed. The deal underscores Shield AI's ambition to build not just an autonomous flight platform, but a comprehensive technology stack spanning simulation, training, and live operational autonomy.

The centerpiece of Shield AI's rise is Hivemind, the company's flagship AI autonomy software. In February, Hivemind was selected by the U.S. Air Force as a software provider for its Collaborative Combat Aircraft program — a next-generation initiative to develop autonomous drone wingmen capable of flying alongside and supporting crewed fighter jets. Notably, Hivemind was chosen to operate in conjunction with Anduril's "Fury" autonomous fighter jet, meaning two competing defense tech companies will share responsibilities across the Air Force's drone stack. The Pentagon's deliberate decision to avoid locking into a single vendor signals both the maturation of the autonomous aircraft market and a growing recognition that no single company currently holds a monopoly on the technology required to operate the next generation of warfighter drones.

For Anduril, the arrangement is unlikely to cause concern. The company, which builds its own autonomy software called Lattice, last raised $2.5 billion at a $30.5 billion valuation in June and is widely reported to be pursuing another fundraise of up to $8 billion at a $60 billion valuation. The two companies represent the leading edge of a new generation of defense technology firms that have emerged to challenge traditional defense contractors — prioritizing software-driven platforms, rapid iteration, and AI-native architecture over the hardware-first approach that has defined legacy defense procurement for decades.

Shield AI's latest raise arrives amid a sustained surge in defense technology investment. Geopolitical instability, accelerating drone warfare across multiple conflict zones, and U.S. government emphasis on modernizing its autonomous systems capabilities have collectively made defense tech one of the most actively funded categories in venture capital. Additional investors participating in Shield AI's Series G include Snowpoint Ventures, InnovationX, Riot Ventures, Disruptive, and Apandion.

Shield AI's trajectory reflects a broader shift in how the defense establishment is thinking about the future of air power. Autonomous systems — capable of operating in contested environments without risking human pilots — are increasingly viewed as essential to maintaining strategic advantage. With Hivemind now embedded in one of the U.S. Air Force's flagship modernization programs, Shield AI has secured a position at the center of that transition. The valuation milestone, and the scale of capital behind it, suggest investors believe that position is only likely to grow more strategically significant in the years ahead.
