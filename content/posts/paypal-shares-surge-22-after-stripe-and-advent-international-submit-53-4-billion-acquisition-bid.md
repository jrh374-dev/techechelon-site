PayPal shares climbed 22% this week after a joint bid from Stripe and Advent International valued the payments company at $53.4 billion, underscoring the continued consolidation appetite in the fintech sector even as broader technology stocks endured a punishing stretch.

The all-cash offer, priced at $60.50 per share, was reported Thursday. PayPal stock jumped 17% on Wednesday alone following the news, pushing the company's relative strength index to 76.4 — well into overbought territory, according to a screen of S&P 500 names conducted by market analysts.

The bid pairs Stripe, the privately held payments infrastructure company, with Advent International, a private equity firm, in what would represent one of the largest fintech acquisitions on record if completed.

The broader market context in which the offer surfaced was notably turbulent. The S&P 500 posted a weekly loss of 1.6%, while the iShares Semiconductor ETF shed more than 10% over the same period as investor confidence in artificial intelligence spending wavered.

That skepticism was partly triggered by reports of delays in Alphabet's latest AI model, as well as Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing's announcement that it was raising its spending forecasts — moves that together prompted a broad selloff in technology shares.

Against that backdrop, several AI-adjacent names finished the week deep in oversold territory. Oracle led that group with a relative strength index of just 17.4, having shed 10% on the week and hitting a new 52-week low on Friday. The company has been raising capital aggressively to fund its AI infrastructure buildout, announcing last month plans to raise $40 billion through a combination of debt and equity financing. Oracle also reported nearly $24 billion in negative free cash flow for the fiscal year. Despite those pressures, 35 of 44 analysts tracked by LSEG rated the stock a buy as of the latest available data.

Super Micro Computer also appeared on the oversold list, with an RSI of 25.3 after falling more than 14% on the week. The server manufacturer, which has been caught in the crosscurrents of softening AI spending sentiment, announced last month that it planned to raise $7 billion through equity-related financing deals to cover hardware component costs. Of the 22 analysts covering the stock, 13 gave it a hold rating, according to LSEG.

The steepest single-week decline among major names belonged to International Business Machines, which lost 26% after issuing a disappointing preliminary earnings report on Tuesday. IBM posted adjusted earnings of $2.93 per share on revenue of $17.2 billion, missing analyst expectations. Shares fell 25% on Tuesday alone — their worst single session on record.

Not every company finished the week under pressure. Cintas, the uniform and facilities services firm, carried an RSI of 77.2 after climbing nearly 14%. The company beat analyst expectations on both earnings and revenue when it reported fourth-quarter results Wednesday, and received an additional lift Thursday when Bank of America upgraded the stock to buy from neutral. Analyst sentiment on Cintas was evenly divided, with 10 analysts recommending a buy, 10 a hold, and one a sell, according to LSEG.

The divergence between PayPal's acquisition-driven rally and the broad technology selloff highlights a market environment where company-specific catalysts — rather than sector momentum — are increasingly determining which stocks move in which direction. Whether the Stripe-Advent bid ultimately closes, and at what terms, will likely set the tone for fintech deal activity through the remainder of 2026.
