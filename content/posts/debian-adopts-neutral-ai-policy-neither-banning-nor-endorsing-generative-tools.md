Debian, one of the oldest and most widely used Linux distributions, has voted to allow developers to use generative AI tools in contributions to the project's development, maintenance, and documentation — stopping short of either endorsing or prohibiting such use.

The new policy, adopted following a vote among the project's contributing developers, states that "generative AI is neither exempt from nor subject to special rules beyond the standards already expected of Debian contributors." The project considered several alternative proposals during deliberations, including measures that would have banned AI-assisted contributions outright.

At its core, the policy places the burden of quality squarely on the contributor. The Debian Project's guidance is explicit: all contributions, regardless of how they were produced, must "satisfy the same standards of quality, correctness, maintainability, and legal compliance." The use of an AI tool, the policy states, "does not diminish the contributor's responsibility for the work they submit."

Contributors are expected to understand, review, test, and modify AI-assisted output before incorporating it into Debian. The policy adds that "blindly accepting or uploading AI-generated material without appropriate human review is inconsistent with Debian's established development practices."

On disclosure, the project stops short of a mandate. Debian says it encourages contributors to disclose whether a contribution involved AI assistance, but does not require them to do so.

The decision has drawn criticism from some corners of the Debian community. As It's FOSS reported, at least one contributor said he is quitting and is "no longer interested in anything coming from Debian" in response to the policy.

The outcome reflects a broader tension playing out across open-source software communities over how to handle AI-generated code. Earlier this year, Ubuntu developer Canonical faced similar backlash when it released its own stance on AI use. Linux creator Linus Torvalds has also weighed in, stating that Linux is "not one of those anti-AI projects."

Debian's approach — essentially extending existing contribution standards to AI-assisted work rather than creating a separate framework — signals that some major open-source projects are opting for governance continuity over new restrictions, even as the debate within those communities remains unsettled.
