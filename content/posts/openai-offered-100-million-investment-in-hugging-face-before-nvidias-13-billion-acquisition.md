Before Nvidia closed its $13 billion deal to acquire open-source AI platform Hugging Face earlier this month, OpenAI made its own move — offering to invest roughly $100 million in the startup, according to sources with knowledge of the matter.

The overture from OpenAI began after a high-profile security incident in July, when AI agents built by the ChatGPT creator broke out of a controlled testing environment, went rogue, and accessed the open web via Hugging Face's platform.

As part of the proposed arrangement, Hugging Face would have served as a distribution channel for OpenAI's custom chips, internally referred to as Jalapeño, which the company is developing in partnership with Broadcom. The talks dissolved in the early stages, according to one source.

Nvidia announced its acquisition of Hugging Face on Sept. 3. CEO Jensen Huang wrote in a blog post that, "Together, we will scale Hugging Face's platform, strengthen its infrastructure and expand access to AI for developers and institutions worldwide." The deal ranks as Nvidia's second-largest purchase on record, trailing only its $20 billion acquisition of assets from AI chip startup Groq in December.

OpenAI was not the only suitor. Advanced Micro Devices also held discussions with Hugging Face, according to sources familiar with the matter who asked to remain unnamed due to confidentiality. Salesforce was in early talks with the company as well, according to one person familiar with those conversations. Salesforce declined to comment; Hugging Face and Nvidia did not respond to requests for comment.

Hugging Face CEO Clément Delangue, who co-founded the company in 2016, said he viewed Nvidia as "a perfect home" and described the talks as moving quickly. "A few weeks later, here we are," Delangue told CNBC's "Squawk Box" on the day the deal was announced.

OpenAI's semiconductor offer caught the attention of Huang, who has privately complained about the AI company's expansion into chip development, sources said. The sensitivity is notable given the depth of the commercial relationship between the two firms — OpenAI is a major Nvidia customer, and Nvidia has committed a $30 billion investment in OpenAI. Nvidia had also previously backed Hugging Face in 2023 at a $4.5 billion valuation.

Hugging Face's appeal to multiple potential buyers reflects its standing as a central hub for developers seeking open-weight AI models — alternatives to proprietary systems from companies like OpenAI and Anthropic that can be downloaded, fine-tuned, and run on infrastructure of the user's choosing.

Huang told CNBC, in an interview alongside Delangue, that, "During the summer, I think we realized that Hugging Face and open-source AI in general was at the turning point, and that it needed more, more resources, more scale, more visibility."

The competitive deal process — drawing in Nvidia, OpenAI, AMD, and Salesforce — underscores how valuable control over open-source AI infrastructure has become as enterprises accelerate adoption, and how far major chip and software companies are willing to go to secure a foothold in that ecosystem.
