Krak, the payments app affiliate of crypto exchange Kraken, launched a U.S. debit card on Tuesday that offers up to 2% cash or bitcoin back, positioning the company in direct competition with mainstream fintech platforms as the broader crypto industry pushes deeper into consumer finance.

The card, called the Krak Card, is issued through a partner bank and allows customers to spend directly from more than 600 currencies and crypto assets, with balances automatically converted to dollars at the point of purchase. Rewards are paid as cash rather than points, with the cashback rate scaling based on the total value of assets a customer holds on the platform.

Users can designate the order in which their assets are drawn down and can split a single transaction across multiple balances — features designed to give crypto-native users fine-grained control over their spending.

The launch puts Krak in more direct competition with Block's Cash App, PayPal's Venmo, SoFi, Robinhood, and Chime, all of which are competing to serve as the primary financial hub for everyday consumers.

"People have lost trust in how the financial system treats them, in the rates they're paid, the fees they're charged, and how rewards actually work. They're right to," said Arjun Sethi, Co-CEO of Kraken parent Payward, in a statement.

Sethi framed the card as a response to what he called a longstanding structural imbalance in consumer finance. "For decades the system has run on a quiet subsidy: ordinary people's deposits, fees, and spending fund the returns, and those returns rarely flow back to them," he said. "The Krak Card turns whatever people choose to hold into money they can spend anywhere, and pays the value back as cash, not points."

A national survey of more than 2,000 U.S. adults, commissioned by Krak, found that 63% of Americans say they feel financially behind, while 60% would switch to a debit card that offers meaningful rewards without requiring them to carry debt.

The 2% reward rate presents a structural challenge, however. Because the Krak Card is issued through a partner bank, it is subject to U.S. limits on debit card interchange fees — the revenue generated each time a customer swipes — which constrains how much can be funded through transaction volume alone. That dynamic suggests Kraken's primary objective is less about interchange revenue and more about deepening its share of each customer's overall financial activity, including deposits and assets held on the platform.

Kraken, founded in 2011, has historically served crypto natives — institutions, active traders, and leverage traders — rather than the casual retail investor. Coinbase has positioned itself as the entry point for new crypto users, while Robinhood has targeted first-time investors more broadly.

Rather than pivoting away from its crypto-native roots as the industry has matured, Kraken appears to be leaning into them. The company earlier this year became the first crypto firm to secure a Federal Reserve master account, allowing its banking subsidiary to connect directly to core U.S. payment rails. It also holds a Wyoming bank charter and offers spot and derivatives trading, institutional custody, and tokenized equities.

As the broader crypto industry shifts away from speculative token trading and toward tokenized stocks, commodities, and dollar-denominated products, Kraken's debit card launch signals a strategy of using its infrastructure advantage to capture more of the financial lives of the customers it already serves — and to attract new ones who have never traded a single coin.
