Southeast Asia has reached 243 million generative AI users in less than three years, yet the region's path to AI-driven revenue runs largely through businesses rather than consumer subscriptions, according to the annual e-Conomy SEA report released Wednesday by Google, Temasek, and Bain & Company.

That user figure represents roughly 58% of the region's working population, with adoption occurring four times faster than the earlier uptake of mobile internet.

Despite that scale, only 1% to 2% of the region's population pays for personal AI subscriptions — a figure that underscores the gap between widespread adoption and direct consumer spending.

"AI monetization does not depend on us paying," Florian Hoppe, a partner at Bain & Company, told CNBC in an interview.

Hoppe said businesses have found alternative routes to extract commercial value from AI, including improvements to e-commerce conversion rates and the automation of routine customer inquiries. Consumers, he added, may be paying for AI indirectly through products developed using the technology.

One example is Shopee, the Southeast Asian e-commerce platform, which now handles approximately 80% of customer queries through AI chatbots — a shift that has cut its cost per customer contact by 30% year on year.

Early case studies cited in the report show AI helping businesses boost conversion rates by 10% year on year and increase the average order value by as much as 75%. In online media, Hoppe pointed to a "significant uplift" in advertising click-through rates and conversions through AI-powered targeting.

Beyond retail, the technology is being applied across ride-hailing, food delivery, travel planning, and digital advertising, reflecting its broadening commercial footprint in the region.

The report also highlights a distinct consumer disposition. Hoppe described Southeast Asian users as holding a "pragmatic optimism" toward AI, embracing the technology for practical benefits. The region has historically ranked among the most active per-capita users of major digital platforms.

That openness is extending to more autonomous applications: 75% of users in the region said they are willing to let AI agents find the best prices and complete purchases on their behalf, while 62% of respondents said they trust AI tools more than they did a year ago.

Singapore is among the early leaders in adoption within individual markets, Hoppe noted.

Usage frequency, however, still lags significantly behind the United States. AI usage per person in Southeast Asia stands at just 10% of U.S. levels, according to the report — a gap that points to considerable room for deeper engagement.

The findings arrive as Southeast Asia's broader digital economy is projected to grow 17% this year to $368 billion in gross merchandise value, with the market expected to exceed $1 trillion by 2035. The region's online travel market is forecast to reach $59 billion in gross merchandise value this year, while online media revenue — encompassing digital advertising and streaming — is expected to rise 22% to $44 billion. Ride-hailing and food delivery are projected to grow 15% to $40 billion in gross merchandise value.

The combination of rapid adoption, enterprise deployment, and a consumer base willing to engage with autonomous AI tools positions Southeast Asia as a testing ground for AI business models that diverge sharply from the subscription-heavy playbooks dominant in Western markets. Whether that enterprise-led monetization approach translates into durable revenue growth at scale remains a central question for the region's digital economy over the next several years.
