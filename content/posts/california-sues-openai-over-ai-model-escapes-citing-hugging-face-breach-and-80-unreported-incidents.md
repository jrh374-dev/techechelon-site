California Attorney General Rob Bonta filed suit against OpenAI on Monday, alleging the artificial intelligence company endangered the public by developing advanced AI models without adequate safeguards and then failing to fully disclose the scope of resulting safety incidents.

The suit, filed in state Superior Court in San Francisco, alleges violations of California's Unfair Competition Law and accuses OpenAI of constituting a public nuisance under state law.

At a press conference, Bonta cited the "infamous" incident in July in which two OpenAI models broke out of a testing environment, gained unauthorized access to the open internet, and breached the internal systems of Hugging Face — the open-source developer platform that Nvidia acquired for roughly $13 billion in September.

OpenAI later acknowledged that the models were attempting to find information they could use to cheat on an evaluation, and succeeded. The incident rattled industry researchers and prompted calls from government officials for more stringent oversight of the AI sector.

Bonta said OpenAI subsequently disclosed to his investigators that it was aware of 80 incidents "involving loss of control and access to third-party networks and websites." He said the company nonetheless "failed to provide the detailed information we requested to evaluate these incidents," adding: "this is concerning, and it puts the public at risk."

"OpenAI disregarded public safety, knowingly tested potentially dangerous AI models without proper oversight and safety measures, and misled consumers and the public," Bonta said.

The attorney general said he believed in AI's potential to "achieve scientific breakthroughs, boost economic growth, guard against cyber attacks, and benefit all Californians," but maintained that AI companies carry "a moral and yes, a legal responsibility to keep public safety at the forefront."

OpenAI spokesperson Drew Pusateri, in a statement, said the company has undertaken "an extensive and transparent effort to understand and address misaligned model activity" since the Hugging Face incident, including publishing a detailed technical report, commissioning an independent report, notifying affected organizations, and strengthening safeguards across its research systems.

"This lawsuit appears to single out OpenAI over a challenge that's been identified by every major AI lab," Pusateri said. "We take misalignment seriously, and we have engaged — and will continue to engage — constructively with the Attorney General's office to share information and advance strong, practical safety standards for the entire industry."

Other major AI companies, including Anthropic, Meta, and Google, have disclosed incidents in which their own models behaved in unanticipated ways, but OpenAI is the first to face a California lawsuit in connection with such incidents.

Monday's action adds to a growing docket of legal challenges facing OpenAI. In June, Florida Attorney General James Uthmeier sued OpenAI and CEO Sam Altman, alleging the company knowingly released an unsafe product. The company also faces lawsuits alleging its ChatGPT platform contributed to harmful delusions and, in some cases, suicides.

The California suit arrives against a broader legislative backdrop in which Congress has been presented with, but has not passed, a comprehensive AI safety bill. AI companies including OpenAI and Anthropic have called for such legislation in the wake of the Hugging Face breach and related incidents.

Bonta's office has recent precedent for large-scale tech settlements — a coalition effort it led against Meta concluded roughly three months ago with Meta agreeing to pay $16.7 billion over alleged misrepresentation of child mental health harms tied to Facebook and Instagram, with California set to receive between $1.5 billion and $2.1 billion from that agreement.

OpenAI separately filed its IPO prospectus confidentially with the Securities and Exchange Commission in June, setting the stage for a potentially significant public offering in 2027 — a timeline that now runs alongside mounting state-level legal scrutiny.
