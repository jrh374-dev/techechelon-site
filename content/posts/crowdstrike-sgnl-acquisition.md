CrowdStrike announced Friday it has agreed to acquire SGNL, the identity security startup, for $740 million in cash. The deal, which is expected to close in the third quarter pending regulatory approval, is the largest acquisition CrowdStrike has made since its 2021 buyout of Humio.

SGNL builds policy-based access management — a category that has quietly become essential as identity has emerged as the dominant attack vector in enterprise breaches. The startup's last public round, an $80 million Series C closed in late 2024, valued it at roughly $480 million, meaning the deal represents a 1.5x premium to the most recent private mark.

## Filling a two-year-old gap

CrowdStrike has been pitching identity as a top priority for nearly two years, but the company's organic offering has lagged what competitors like Palo Alto Networks and SentinelOne built out earlier. The SGNL acquisition closes that gap mechanically, but raises a harder question: can a stitched-together identity layer compete with platforms that built theirs natively?

Analysts at Wedbush were skeptical in a Friday morning note, calling the deal "necessary, not visionary." Shares of CrowdStrike were down 1.4 percent in early trading.

## What it signals for the sector

The bigger story is that the consolidation phase of identity security has clearly begun. Three of the top four endpoint vendors have now made identity acquisitions in the last 18 months. The independents are running out of time, and their valuations reflect it.
