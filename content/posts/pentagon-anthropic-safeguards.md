The Pentagon has spent the last six weeks pressuring Anthropic to loosen the safety constraints on its frontier models for use in classified defense systems, according to four people familiar with the negotiations. Anthropic has refused, setting up the first public test of the model-deployment carve-outs written into the Department of Defense's AI framework last year.

The dispute centers on a single contract — a roughly $200 million annual agreement for Claude-family models to be deployed inside classified networks for intelligence analysis and operational planning. The Pentagon's contracting office wants the models to operate without the standard refusal behaviors that Anthropic ships in commercial deployments.

## What the carve-outs were supposed to do

The DoD's 2025 AI framework included language allowing "operational necessity" exemptions to commercial-model safety constraints. At the time, the language was understood by industry to apply to narrow use cases: target identification, hostage rescue planning, and similar high-stakes operational moments. The current dispute reportedly involves much broader categories.

Anthropic's position, according to three of the four people, is that loosening the constraints would violate the company's published responsible-scaling policy and create a category of model behavior that cannot be cleanly audited. The Pentagon's position is that the company knew what classified deployment would require when it bid.

## Why this matters

The decision will set the precedent for every frontier-model defense contract that follows. If Anthropic holds the line, other labs will follow. If the Pentagon prevails, the commercial-deployment standards that the AI labs have spent two years building will quietly become optional inside the defense ecosystem.
