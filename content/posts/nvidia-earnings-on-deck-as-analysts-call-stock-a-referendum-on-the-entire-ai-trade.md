Nvidia is set to report second-quarter earnings after the bell Wednesday, and market watchers say the results carry implications well beyond a single company's bottom line — positioning the chipmaker's quarterly release as a de facto verdict on the durability of the AI infrastructure boom.

Wall Street projects earnings per share of $2.09 on $92.28 billion in revenue, according to FactSet. Nvidia carries a market capitalization of more than $5 trillion, making it the largest member of the S&P 500 and, by extension, a weight that can move the broader index in either direction.

Nvidia shares rose roughly 2% on Tuesday, snapping a seven-session losing streak — the stock's longest such decline since September 2022. Even after that skid, shares remain up around 14% in 2026. The stock closed Tuesday at a level just under 10% below its all-time closing high of nearly $236 per share, set in mid-May.

Jim Cramer, host of CNBC's "Mad Money," said Tuesday that Nvidia has grown so deeply embedded in the AI ecosystem that its earnings effectively function as a referendum on the trade itself. "Nvidia's truly become anything but ordinary, it's all-important, and it's the ultimate battleground," Cramer said.

Cramer pointed to the breadth of Nvidia's exposure — data centers, memory chips, and the capital spending plans of the world's largest technology companies — as the reason a single quarterly report can reverberate so widely. "Never before has there been a company with so many tentacles in so many segments of the economy," he said.

Despite that, Cramer cautioned that even a strong quarter might not resolve the debate surrounding the stock. He cited several headwinds outside Nvidia's direct control: political opposition that could slow data center construction, shortages of high-bandwidth memory that he said are already costing the company sales, and U.S. government export restrictions that have curtailed Nvidia's ability to sell advanced chips in China.

Scrutiny has also focused on Nvidia's growing investment portfolio across the AI ecosystem. Critics have argued the structure of some deals is circular — that Nvidia is funding businesses that could use the capital to purchase its own products. Cramer pushed back on that framing. "Why shouldn't Nvidia use its profits to help ensure that it remains on top?" he said.

Moses Ventures founder Danny Moses, one of the investors chronicled in "The Big Short" for betting against mortgage-backed securities ahead of the 2008 financial crisis, told CNBC's "Fast Money" on Tuesday that the "two most important assets right now to the market are the 10-year bond and Nvidia." Moses said he is bullish on the stock and expects the company to beat estimates and guide higher.

The average analyst polled by LSEG holds a buy rating on Nvidia and anticipates roughly 40% in upside over the next 12 months, reflecting the broad consensus that demand for the company's chips remains robust even as hyperscaler customers develop alternative silicon.

The bond market adds another layer of complexity heading into Wednesday. The U.S. 10-year Treasury note yielded 4.623% on Tuesday, though yields fell broadly on the day, with the 10-year losing nearly 8 basis points. That followed a stretch in which 30-year bond rates hit levels not seen in nearly 20 years. The monthly U.S. budget deficit topped $432 billion in July, its highest since March 2021, and U.S. government debt surpassed $40 trillion as of last week. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said his department would double debt buybacks in an effort to steady long-dated yields.

Also on the calendar Wednesday: July's personal consumption expenditures price index — the Federal Reserve's preferred inflation gauge — is due at 8:30 a.m. ET. Economists polled by Dow Jones forecast a month-over-month increase of 0.1% and a year-over-year gain of 3.6%.

With Nvidia's earnings, the PCE print, and Federal Reserve Chairman Kevin Warsh's scheduled address Friday at the Jackson Hole symposium all converging in a narrow window, investors enter the back half of the week facing an unusually concentrated set of market-moving catalysts.
