Apple kicked off its 2026 Worldwide Developers Conference on Monday with a keynote in Cupertino, California, putting artificial intelligence, Siri updates, and a landmark leadership handoff at the center of the annual event.

The conference, which runs through June 12, opened with a presentation by outgoing CEO Tim Cook alongside other Apple executives, covering new software for iPhones, iPads, Mac computers, Apple Watches, and other devices.

The keynote marks the last WWDC keynote Cook will lead as chief executive. Apple announced on April 20 that Senior Vice President of Hardware Engineering John Ternus will succeed Cook, becoming the company's eighth CEO. Cook will transition to the role of executive chairman on September 1.

Ternus, who has spent 25 years at Apple, is expected to use the conference as an early opportunity to speak directly to developers, employees, and the company's broader base of supporters.

Cook succeeded Steve Jobs in 2011, shortly before Jobs' death, and has led the company through more than a decade of product expansion and revenue growth.

The central question heading into this year's WWDC is whether Apple can demonstrate a credible AI strategy after years of slow progress on that front. Apple Intelligence, the company's AI platform, has been "one of the big black eyes" of Cook's tenure, according to Dan Newman, CEO of The Futurum Group.

Newman told CNBC that WWDC represents Cook's final opportunity to signal a turning point in Apple's AI posture before Ternus formally takes the reins.

Apple is expected to reveal further details about its partnership with Google to integrate Gemini models into its ecosystem — an arrangement first announced in January. The company's stock has approached record highs in recent weeks, reflecting investor and analyst approval of that partnership, along with continued strong sales momentum for the latest iPhone lineup.

The software updates unveiled at WWDC are not expected to reach consumers immediately. Apple typically releases new hardware in the fall, and Monday's software announcements are expected to launch alongside those devices.

Beyond AI, Apple will cover updates to iOS, iPadOS, macOS, watchOS, visionOS, and tvOS during the week-long event. A more technical "Platforms State of the Union" session was also scheduled for Monday.

Apple needs to persuade developers that Siri and the Apple Intelligence platform are worth building for — a challenge that has grown more pointed as competitors have accelerated their own AI integrations.

The keynote was available to stream on YouTube, at apple.com, and through the Apple TV app.

How Apple frames its AI roadmap under Cook — and how Ternus positions himself for the transition — will likely shape investor and developer sentiment heading into the fall product cycle.
