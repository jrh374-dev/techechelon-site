LeBron James signaled an upcoming commercial partnership with prediction market platform Polymarket on Saturday, posting a 14-second video on X that appeared to be an advertisement for the company.

In the clip, the basketball legend appears in the elevator of Polymarket's headquarters and heads to the company's sports floor. "Welcome to Polymarket HQ. Coming soon. In partnership with @Polymarket," James wrote alongside the post.

The teaser points to a larger campaign focused on football, according to a person familiar with the matter who spoke on condition of anonymity because they were not authorized to speak publicly. Additional details are expected in the coming week, the person said. Financial terms of the arrangement have not been disclosed. Representatives for both James and Polymarket declined to comment beyond the Saturday post.

James, the NBA's all-time leading scorer, announced in July that he would join the Philadelphia 76ers for his record-extending 24th season. ESPN previously reported he signed a two-year, $8 million contract with a player option — a significant reduction from the more than $50 million he earned with the Los Angeles Lakers last season. The 2026-2027 NBA season is set to begin in mid-October.

NBA rules restrict players' financial interests in companies that offer league-related wagering or similar products, including limits on ownership stakes. The league's rules will likely shape the structure of any formal agreement.

James joins a growing roster of athletes and sports organizations aligning with prediction market platforms. Earlier this year, Miami Heat star Giannis Antetokounmpo became a shareholder in rival platform Kalshi and agreed to participate in marketing and live events — though Kalshi has said his stake is passive and its rules prohibit him from trading on NBA-related markets.

Polymarket has separately inked deals with Major League Soccer, Major League Baseball, and the National Hockey League, as well as individual franchises including MLB's New York Yankees and NHL's New York Rangers.

The announcement comes as Polymarket reorganizes its marketing operation ahead of a busy fall sports calendar. The company recently hired Travis VanderZanden — formerly of Uber Technologies and Lyft, and the founder of e-scooter startup Bird — as its chief growth officer, with oversight of the company's marketing function.

Prediction markets allow users to trade contracts tied to the outcome of future events, including sporting contests. The sector has attracted increasing interest from athletes, leagues, and investors as the platforms expand beyond their politically focused roots into mainstream sports entertainment.

With one of the most recognizable names in professional sports now attached to Polymarket's brand, the platform appears to be positioning itself for a significant push into football-season audiences — a period that historically draws peak engagement for sports-adjacent financial products.
