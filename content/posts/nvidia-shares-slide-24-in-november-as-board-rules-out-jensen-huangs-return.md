Nvidia shares have fallen nearly 24% this month as of Tuesday morning, putting the chipmaker on pace for its worst monthly decline since April 2022, when the stock plunged 32%. The drop follows Jensen Huang's resignation as CEO last Thursday after reports emerged that he had multiple affairs, including one with a woman U.S. intelligence reportedly believes to be a Chinese spy.

Huang informed employees of one of the affairs in an email on November 8. The company's board announced his departure shortly after. A person familiar with the matter told CNBC that Nvidia's board is not considering bringing Huang back in any capacity.

Nvidia shares were up roughly 1% in premarket trading Tuesday ahead of the company's quarterly earnings report, due after the market close — a modest recovery following days of steep declines.

Colette Kress, Nvidia's chief financial officer, is expected to face questions about the leadership transition and the company's near-term outlook when the report drops later in the session.

In a separate disclosure, Nvidia revealed in a regulatory filing that it trimmed its stake in Intel by 25% during the quarter ending September 30, selling nearly $5.5 billion in shares. The move reduces Nvidia's holding in Intel to 161 million shares from 215 million. Intel shares slipped on the news.

Morgan Stanley analyst Erik Woodring noted that heading into the quarter, Nvidia "was the most 'under-owned' large-cap tech stock exiting the quarter." Woodring said the gap between Nvidia's S&P 500 weighting and institutional ownership widened quarter-over-quarter by 22 basis points, ending the third quarter of 2026 at negative 2.75% — which he described as an all-time high.

"Given NVDA shares have fallen 32% in the last 45 days vs. AAPL up 6%, it's highly plausible Apple could be the most under-owned mega-cap tech stock at present," Woodring said.

The broader market was under pressure as well. The Dow Jones Industrial Average slid more than 700 points, or 1.4%, on Monday. The S&P 500 lost 1.9%, while the Nasdaq Composite dropped 2.5% — its steepest single-day decline since November 5.

On Tuesday, Dow futures were off 234 points, or 0.5%, while Nasdaq-100 futures advanced 0.5% and S&P 500 futures hovered near the flatline.

Ben Emons, founder of FedWatch Advisors, attributed part of the market's unease to Nvidia's situation. "The tape feels increasingly fragile as Nvidia's AI premium continues to unwind and investors reassess the durability of the ecosystem built around its dominance," he wrote in a note. "This backdrop has fueled the 'dispersion trade,' gradually eroding the extreme concentration that has defined the S&P 500 and Nasdaq."

Home Depot added to the Dow's drag Tuesday after announcing that Richard McPhail would succeed Ted Decker as CEO effective next week. Decker, who will remain as chairman, has been on medical leave since August. Home Depot shares fell nearly 2% despite the company reporting quarterly results that beat analyst expectations.

Disney also reported a fiscal fourth-quarter earnings beat, posting adjusted earnings of $1.77 per share on revenue of $25.95 billion, against analyst forecasts of $1.67 per share and $25.18 billion in revenue, according to estimates from LSEG. The company said it is targeting $3 billion in cost cuts by fiscal 2029. Disney shares ticked higher in premarket trading before retreating about 1%.

Nvidia's earnings report after Tuesday's close will be the first since Huang's exit and is likely to serve as a key test of investor confidence in the company's ability to maintain its dominant position in AI chip supply chains without its longtime leader at the helm.
