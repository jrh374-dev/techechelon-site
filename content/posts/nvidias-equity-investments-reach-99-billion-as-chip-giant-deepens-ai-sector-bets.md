Nvidia's equity investments reached $99 billion as of July 26, up from roughly $7 billion a year earlier and approximately $2.2 billion two years prior, underscoring the chip giant's rapid transformation into one of the largest corporate backers of technology companies in the world.

The company has committed more than $40 billion in 2026 alone, with capital flowing across frontier AI laboratories, cloud infrastructure providers, and companies developing emerging hardware technologies, according to the company's earnings disclosures.

The scale now places Nvidia near the top of strategic tech investors globally, though it still trails Alphabet and Amazon, both of which reported equity investment portfolios worth more than $100 billion in recent earnings.

Nvidia Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress told analysts on an earnings call that the company had invested "nearly $50 billion in the frontier AI labs." She noted that while these labs faced "extraordinary" demand for compute, they were growing faster than their balance sheets and credit profiles could support and struggled to secure AI factory infrastructure on their own. "Nvidia is needed to help power this flywheel," she said.

The company's most prominent disclosed commitment came in February, when Nvidia said it would invest $30 billion into OpenAI as part of OpenAI's $110 billion funding round. Nvidia has also provided up to $105 billion of conditional credit support for an OpenAI data center in Ohio.

Infrastructure-focused "neoclouds" — companies that purchase Nvidia GPUs and rent access to other firms — have also drawn significant capital. Nvidia invested $2 billion into CoreWeave in January and announced a $2 billion investment in Nebius in March.

Ian Fogg, research director at CCS Insight, said Nvidia's investment strategy serves a dual purpose. "Nvidia has a clear interest in ensuring that its customers and partners prosper to provide future business for Nvidia," Fogg told CNBC. "Equity investments help companies to innovate, but also give Nvidia a degree of control to encourage companies to take a Nvidia-related innovation path."

The company has also directed at least $6.5 billion since March into photonics and optical technology companies, which use light rather than electricity to transmit data. Lumentum, Coherent, and Marvell each received $2 billion investments. A separate $1 billion equity stake was taken in Nokia, targeting the telecom market.

Nvidia's $5 billion investment in Intel has grown to a value of $30 billion, while its SpaceX holding was valued at $21 billion as of June.

Naveen Chhabra, principal analyst at Forrester, said the Intel position carries supply-chain logic. "As global AI chip demand runs into physical supply constraints particularly around high-bandwidth memory and advanced packaging, Nvidia uses strategic equity positions like in domestic manufacturing options like Intel, to secure priority manufacturing access, reduce Asian foundry concentration risk and stabilize key component supplies," Chhabra told CNBC.

Chhabra added that investments in optical networking specialists such as Coherent are designed to protect Nvidia's software architecture. "This creates high switching costs and protects the CUDA software moat against competing accelerators from AMD or internal custom chips from cloud providers," he said.

Nvidia's own financial results reflect the demand environment driving these decisions. The company's revenue reached $96.2 billion in its fiscal second quarter, a 106% increase year over year, with $48.7 billion of that total coming from its Hyperscale segment. The stock has risen 33% over the past 12 months.

With more than $500 billion in financing partnerships announced in August and a $12.9 billion planned acquisition of AI startup Hugging Face disclosed Thursday, Nvidia's capital deployment strategy signals that the chip giant intends to shape the broader AI ecosystem — not merely supply it.
