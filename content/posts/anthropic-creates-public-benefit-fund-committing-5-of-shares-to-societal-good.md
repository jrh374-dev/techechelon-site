Anthropic announced Sunday that it is establishing a "public benefit fund" funded with 5% of the company's shares, framing the move as a response to growing public skepticism about the artificial intelligence industry.

The fund will be structured as a wholly-owned entity within Anthropic, according to the company, giving it "increased flexibility and scale" to support projects aimed at societal benefit.

The announcement, [first reported by TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/18/anthropic-says-its-committing-5-of-its-shares-to-a-public-benefit-fund), includes a provision allowing Anthropic to contribute an additional 2% of its shares for every 50% increase in the company's valuation.

Anthropic outlined broad categories for how the fund's resources might be deployed, including "large donations to traditional nonprofits or direct distributions to specific populations." The company also pointed to AI's potential impact on employment as an area of focus — a concern to which it has already committed $200 million in separate research funding.

"Given the pace of AI progress, bolder approaches may be needed," the company said in a statement. "The Fund will have the versatility to pursue them."

The announcement comes as the broader AI sector faces what Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei has characterized as "fundamentally a crisis of trust." Public sentiment toward the industry has grown more negative even as AI investment and deployment have accelerated, pressuring major players to demonstrate alignment between commercial ambitions and public welfare.

Anthropic did not name the individuals who will govern the fund or outline a formal decision-making process for allocating resources. The company said it would share additional details on structure and governance in the coming months.

The timing of the announcement coincides with a turbulent week for AI-linked equities. The Nasdaq fell 4.5% last week — its worst weekly performance since June — driven in part by investor rotation out of AI stocks amid hotter-than-expected inflation data. September's consumer price index rose 3.7% year over year, above the 3.6% consensus estimate.

Anthropic's move mirrors a broader pattern among AI laboratories of pairing aggressive commercial expansion with public-facing commitments intended to address concerns about the technology's societal effects.

Whether the fund's structure will satisfy critics who have called for more binding oversight mechanisms remains to be seen, and the absence of governance details at launch is likely to draw scrutiny from researchers and policymakers alike.
