Brad Lightcap, one of OpenAI's longest-serving executives and a close confidante of CEO Sam Altman, announced Tuesday he is leaving the company after eight years — the latest departure from a lab that has seen a string of senior exits in recent months.

Lightcap joined OpenAI in 2018 and held several senior roles during his tenure, most notably a four-year stint as the company's operating chief. He stepped back from that role in April and was moved into a position focused on "special projects." At the time, OpenAI said chief revenue officer Denise Dresser would assume most of his responsibilities.

In a post on X on Tuesday, Lightcap said he plans to "start something new." "I feel incredibly fortunate to have spent most of the last decade pursuing our mission and building this company," he wrote. "Sitting here today, mission success feels within sight. It has been the honor of my life to help bring us to this point, and to do it alongside all of you."

Altman responded publicly on X, noting his long history with Lightcap, who predates OpenAI itself. "i very fondly remember our earliest conversations about openai, when it sounded totally crazy and you were one of the few people that got it," Altman wrote. "since then, you have taken on any function and challenge openai has needed; we would not be where we are without you."

Among Lightcap's most tangible contributions was building OpenAI's go-to-market operation. Over an 18-month stretch through mid-2025, he grew that team from roughly 50 people to more than 700, encompassing sales, customer success, developer relations, and strategic partnerships. That expansion began in 2023, coinciding with the launch of GPT-4 and ChatGPT Enterprise, as the company moved to compete with Anthropic in the enterprise market.

Lightcap's exit comes amid a broader wave of departures that has drawn attention to OpenAI's internal stability. Last month, Fidji Simo — who had served as OpenAI's product and business chief — announced she was stepping down to focus on recovery after what she described as a "severe exacerbation of a chronic illness." In April, three additional executives announced they were leaving: Bill Peebles, who led the now-defunct short-form video app Sora; Kevin Weil, vice president of OpenAI for Science; and Srinivas Narayanan, tech chief of B2B Applications.

The cumulative departures arrive at a sensitive moment for the company. OpenAI is currently working to justify a $852 billion valuation while preparing for what is expected to be a significant initial public offering.

"Among the most rewarding parts of this journey for me has been watching each of these teams mature under brilliant leaders," Lightcap wrote in his farewell post. "I am excited for the next decade in the hands of this incredibly capable team."

How OpenAI manages its leadership continuity in the months ahead — particularly as it navigates the pressures of a pending IPO — will be closely watched by investors, enterprise clients, and rivals alike.
