The U.S. Department of Defense has officially added a customized version of OpenAI's ChatGPT to its GenAI.mil portal, making the tool available to more than 3 million department personnel for work involving controlled, unclassified information.

The deployment, announced August 31, follows months of security testing that began in February. An OpenAI official had said in June that the product was expected to launch in "early July," a timeline that slipped by several weeks.

The military-specific product, dubbed ChatGPT Mil, joins Google's Gemini on GenAI.mil, the Pentagon's centralized platform for generative AI tools. The Department launched the portal on December 9 with Gemini as its sole offering. Pentagon officials also signed an agreement around that time to bring Elon Musk's xAI tool to the platform, though no formal deployment of that product has since been announced.

A Defense Department official, speaking before the formal announcement on the condition of anonymity, described the two tools as complementary rather than redundant. "Gemini, I like for search functionality or scraping certain sources. Chat for more, like, text-based work," the official said. "We want to have access to all these different models, so people, if they prefer one for a certain task, they can use it."

A Pentagon press release described ChatGPT Mil as bringing "a familiar commercial experience into the Department's secure environment, tailored to warfighter needs," with intended uses spanning planning, policy, logistics, and administration. An OpenAI official speaking on background suggested operators at Northern Command could use the tool to streamline planning, resource allocation, or disaster response coordination with FEMA. The official also said logisticians at combatant commands would be a natural audience, with uses such as reconciling supply and maintenance updates and drafting transportation requirements.

Both OpenAI and Pentagon officials were explicit in background discussions that the GenAI.mil models are not intended for combat operations. OpenAI noted that ChatGPT's requirements for connectivity and substantial computing power make it an ill fit for battlefield use anyway. The Pentagon has separately argued that it should retain broad latitude over how it uses AI products — a position that has created friction with AI developer Anthropic.

Building out a frontier model for GenAI.mil is an "arduous" process, according to a former defense official involved in such testing. It requires assembling computing resources, managing user security conditions, compiling relevant data, and anticipating user requests — all while satisfying both the Defense Information Systems Agency and the National Security Agency. The former official described NSA's role as probing both for potential external intrusions and for ways the model itself might breach its own containment.

Joe Larson, who leads OpenAI's government sales, said in a statement that the integration is "grounded in our national security principles, pairing access to powerful tools with clear safeguards, meaningful human judgment, and accountability for high-stakes decisions."

The rollout comes as OpenAI faces tightening regulatory scrutiny on another front: the European Commission designated ChatGPT a Very Large Online Search Engine under the EU's Digital Services Act on the same day, giving the company until the end of December 2026 to comply with a new set of transparency and content obligations. The Pentagon deployment, by contrast, reflects a notably different regulatory posture — one oriented around controlled access and institutional integration rather than public-facing restrictions.
