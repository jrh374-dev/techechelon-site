Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co. reported first-quarter revenue of $26.4 billion on Friday, a 35 percent increase year-over-year and $2.6 billion above the consensus analyst estimate. The headline number was strong. The composition was the actual story.

For the first time in the company's history, demand from AI-focused customers — primarily Nvidia, AMD, and a handful of hyperscaler in-house silicon programs — accounted for more than half of all wafer starts. Smartphone chip orders, which dominated TSMC's revenue mix as recently as 2022, were a distant second.

## The capacity dance

Chief executive C.C. Wei told analysts on the earnings call that the company's 2026 capital expenditure budget would be revised upward, to between $48 billion and $52 billion. That is several billion above the prior guidance and reflects a clear bet that AI-driven demand has further to run.

The risk in that bet, as every TSMC analyst note will inevitably mention, is timing. AI compute demand has grown faster than any prior wave of semiconductor demand on record. It has also been concentrated in a narrow customer base. If any single hyperscaler reduces its 2027 orders, TSMC's capacity utilization model collapses quickly.

## Watch the lumpiness

Wei was unusually candid on this point. Asked about quarter-to-quarter visibility, he described the orders as "lumpier than we have ever seen" — large, concentrated, and increasingly project-tied rather than forecast-driven. That is a structural change in the semiconductor industry, and it is not yet priced into anyone's model.
