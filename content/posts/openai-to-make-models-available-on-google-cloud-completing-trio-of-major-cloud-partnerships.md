OpenAI will make its artificial intelligence models available to developers through Google Cloud, the company announced Wednesday, placing its technology on all three of the top U.S. cloud infrastructure providers for the first time.

The move comes less than six months after OpenAI ended Microsoft's exclusive license on its intellectual property. The day after that exclusivity lapsed, Amazon unveiled plans to host OpenAI models — a rollout that began in April. Google is now the third major cloud platform to secure access, bringing OpenAI's GPT products to its model garden and Antigravity coding tool in the coming weeks.

"Google Cloud's longstanding investment in machine learning and AI has made it a destination for AI workloads and the developers and data scientists building them," OpenAI said in a blog post.

For OpenAI, the partnership reflects a deliberate push to broaden distribution of its technology among enterprise developers — a segment the company views as essential as it looks to grow beyond its consumer-facing roots.

Google has accelerated its cloud business considerably this year. Annualized revenue growth in the segment reached approximately 82% in the second quarter, up from 63% in the first quarter, driven partly by demand for AI models and custom chips. By comparison, Amazon and Microsoft posted second-quarter cloud growth of 37% and 42%, respectively. All three companies are scheduled to report quarterly results next week.

Most business spending on AI currently flows to OpenAI and Anthropic, according to data from corporate card issuer Ramp, though some corporate IT departments have integrated Google's Gemini models into their workflows. In September, Google announced its next-generation Gemini 4 Argon model.

The Google partnership extends a relationship that predates the current commercial arrangement. OpenAI said last year that it was already using Google Cloud to run ChatGPT and its application programming interface.

The history between the two companies is layered. OpenAI was founded in 2015 partly as a counterweight to the concentration of AI research at companies like Google. Two years later, Google employees produced the influential Transformer paper that helped enable modern AI systems — but it was OpenAI that brought the technology to mass audiences with ChatGPT.

OpenAI is currently courting investors at a $1.4 trillion valuation while also targeting an initial public offering, and securing broad cloud distribution across Amazon, Microsoft, and Google underscores how central enterprise access has become to its commercial strategy.

With all three major cloud platforms now hosting its models, OpenAI's next challenge will be converting that distribution reach into durable enterprise revenue — and demonstrating that its technology commands a premium even as rivals including Google's own Gemini compete for the same customers.
