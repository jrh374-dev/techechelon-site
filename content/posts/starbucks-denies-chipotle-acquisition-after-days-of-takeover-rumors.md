Starbucks on Monday flatly denied it is pursuing an acquisition of Chipotle Mexican Grill, putting an end — at least for now — to days of speculation about what would have been the largest restaurant industry takeover on record.

"Starbucks is not buying Chipotle," a Starbucks spokesperson told CNBC's Jim Cramer.

Chipotle shares fell nearly 4% in morning trading Monday following the statement, while Starbucks stock was 1% higher, reversing a pattern that had played out during the rumor cycle in the days prior, when Chipotle shares climbed and Starbucks shares declined on the reports.

The denial follows a Financial Times report published Thursday that cited people familiar with the matter, stating that Starbucks had been working with advisors on a Chipotle takeover proposal in recent months.

Earlier in October, Semafor had reported that Chipotle hired advisors to defend against activist investors or an unwanted takeover bid, adding a layer of context to the swirling deal speculation.

Chipotle has been working to recover from a difficult 2025, during which traffic to its restaurants fell. The fast-casual chain's shares are down about 20% over the last year, a decline that made it a more affordable acquisition target in the eyes of analysts watching the situation.

Much of the investor enthusiasm on the Chipotle side centered on Brian Niccol, who previously served as Chipotle's chief executive before leaving to lead Starbucks. A deal would have represented a reunion of sorts between Niccol and the chain he once ran.

Starbucks shareholders, however, were largely uneasy about the prospect. The coffee company is in the middle of its own operational turnaround, and investors expressed concern that a high-cost acquisition would serve as an expensive distraction from that effort.

Both companies now face the task of convincing their respective investor bases that management remains focused. For Starbucks, Monday's denial may ease pressure on its share price in the short term, while Chipotle must continue to demonstrate that its recovery plan can generate renewed customer traffic without the benefit of a buyout premium.

Whether further consolidation interest emerges in the restaurant sector — from Starbucks or another potential acquirer — remains an open question as the industry navigates shifting consumer spending patterns heading into 2027.
