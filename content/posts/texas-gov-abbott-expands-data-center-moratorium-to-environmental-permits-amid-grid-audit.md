Texas Gov. Greg Abbott on Monday ordered the state's environmental regulator to halt all data center-related permits until the Electric Reliability Council of Texas completes an audit of the existing waitlist for data centers seeking to connect to the state's power grid.

The directive expands a moratorium Abbott first imposed in August, which had targeted new data center grid approvals. Monday's order brings the Texas Commission on Environmental Quality under the same pause, making the freeze more comprehensive.

The TCEQ is expected to report back to the governor's office on its compliance by Oct. 19.

Abbott's August moratorium also triggered an audit of the ERCOT waitlist — the same audit now serving as the threshold that must be cleared before permits can resume. The environmental hold effectively adds a second regulatory layer to an industry that had been growing rapidly in the state.

Texas was on track to surpass Virginia as the state with the largest data center market in the world by the end of the decade, according to a February report from JLL. Analysts at BloombergNEF found last month that nearly 20% of the U.S.'s 253-gigawatt data center pipeline would be at risk under Texas's pause — roughly 50 gigawatts of proposed capacity — with potential revenue losses reaching upward of $8 billion by the first quarter of 2027.

A separate report from advocacy group Data Center Watch claimed $68 billion in new data center development was delayed or cancelled last quarter due to local opposition, a figure the group says is now bleeding into financial markets. Anthropic's anticipated IPO is expected to list negative public sentiment toward AI infrastructure as a risk factor in its forthcoming prospectus.

The political dimensions of Abbott's reversal are considerable. As recently as 2025, Abbott described Texas — citing its sales tax exemptions for qualifying data centers and high-profile projects such as OpenAI's Stargate — as the "epicenter of AI development." A press release accompanying the August moratorium stated that "next session, Governor Abbott will work with the Legislature to eliminate any financial incentives for data centers," a stark departure from that earlier posture.

Data centers have emerged as a defining issue in the 2026 midterm elections. A recent NBC News poll found that 64% of Americans would be less likely to vote for a candidate who "supports building local data centers." Abbott, who is running a competitive race for a fourth term against state Rep. Gina Hinojosa, has found himself at the center of that backlash.

The issue is not limited to Texas. Local opposition to data center construction has escalated nationally, reflecting growing public concern about power consumption, water use, and land development tied to AI infrastructure expansion.

With the ERCOT audit still pending and the TCEQ freeze now in place, the pace at which Texas can process new data center development remains uncertain — and the outcome of the audit is likely to shape both the regulatory and political trajectory of the issue heading into November.
