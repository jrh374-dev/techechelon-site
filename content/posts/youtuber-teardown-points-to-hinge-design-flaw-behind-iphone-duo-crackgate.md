A teardown video posted by YouTuber CuriousHagi has surfaced what may be the structural cause behind a wave of cracked screens on Apple's iPhone Duo, the foldable device that launched to significant consumer interest but has since attracted a growing chorus of complaints on social media and the iPhone Duo subreddit.

Reports from users describe the phone failing quickly and in dramatic fashion — in some cases within hours of unboxing. The pattern is consistent: the hinge appears to catch on something, produces an audible crack, and the screen breaks. Tellingly, the damage occurs in the same location across affected devices.

CuriousHagi was able to reproduce the cracked screen during durability testing before disassembling a unit to identify the source of the failure. What the teardown revealed is a hinge mechanism of considerable complexity, comprising a gear-like central hinge and two distinct types of linking hinges.

The component that failed in the teardown is one of the links positioned in the middle of the phone. That particular link is thinner than the links situated above or below it, and it snapped at a small notch — the point of least structural resistance. CuriousHagi also identified marks where the hinge had made contact with the support plate, suggesting that is where the mechanism may be catching before it breaks.

The findings offer a mechanical explanation for why cracks appear in the same place across user reports: if the middle linking hinge is consistently the weakest point, and the geometry of the hinge causes it to catch against the support plate under certain conditions, the failure mode would be both predictable and repeatable.

Apple has not publicly commented on the screen cracking reports or the teardown findings. The company has not issued a service advisory or announced a replacement program as of this writing.

The issue, which users have taken to calling "crackgate," underscores the engineering challenges that accompany foldable display designs. Hinge mechanisms must withstand tens of thousands of open-and-close cycles while maintaining precise tolerances — a task complicated by the need to keep components thin enough to allow a phone to fold flat.

The iPhone Duo's hinge problems arrive alongside a separate durability report from JerryRigEverything, which found that dust could compromise the device, reinforcing concerns about the phone's real-world resilience. Whether Apple addresses the hinge design through a quiet hardware revision, a repair program, or a public acknowledgment remains the central question for the growing number of Duo owners watching the situation unfold.
