TikTok has permanently suspended the social media account used to promote Alex Gibney's documentary about Elon Musk, escalating a months-long pattern of major platforms refusing to carry advertising for the film.

The suspension came just ahead of the documentary's wider release, according to The Hollywood Reporter, which reported that the film's distributor, Bleecker Street, was informed Friday afternoon that a permanent suspension was imminent. TikTok did not respond to a request for comment. Bleecker Street also did not respond to a request for comment from The Verge.

The move deepens the irony surrounding the account's removal — shortly before it was taken down, the account posted a message urging viewers to see the film "before it gets shut down."

TikTok has no official corporate affiliation with Elon Musk. However, part owner Larry Ellison appears in the documentary by way of a text message in which he offered to help fund Musk's acquisition of Twitter.

The ban represents a significant escalation beyond the advertising disputes that surfaced last month, when YouTube, Meta, X, and TikTok all drew criticism for rejecting paid ads for the film. YouTube and Google reversed their decisions, describing the rejections as an "error." TikTok and X did not reverse course, and neither platform has since allowed paid advertisements for the film.

Banning the promotional account entirely goes further than an ad refusal, removing the film's ability to post organic content to TikTok's user base entirely.

The documentary is directed by Alex Gibney, a filmmaker known for investigative nonfiction work, and is distributed by Bleecker Street. The film's social media strategy had leaned heavily on short-form video platforms ahead of its wider release — making TikTok's action particularly disruptive at a critical promotional window.

The episode underscores the considerable leverage that large platforms hold over the promotional infrastructure for films and other media, particularly when platform ownership overlaps with the subjects those films examine. Larry Ellison's stake in TikTok's current ownership structure places him in the unusual position of being both a subject of the documentary and an indirect stakeholder in one of its primary promotional channels.

Neither TikTok nor Bleecker Street has offered a public explanation for the suspension or indicated whether any appeal process is underway. The situation is likely to draw renewed scrutiny over platform content moderation decisions that intersect with the commercial and political interests of their owners.
