Anthropic is considering expanding its initial public offering after closing its order books Tuesday to institutional demand of approximately $400 billion and retail demand approaching $100 billion, according to people familiar with the details who asked not to be named because they were not authorized to speak publicly.

The AI lab is weighing setting its final price well above $80 per share, the people said, with a decision expected after market close Wednesday. The stock is set to begin trading on the Nasdaq under the ticker symbol ANT on Thursday.

Anthropic said last week it would offer 825 million shares at a price range of $70 to $78 per share, which would net up to $64.35 billion at the high end of that range. The company has not disclosed a revised share count or price target for the potential upsizing. A representative for Anthropic declined to comment.

Demand for the offering exceeds what was seen for SpaceX's Nasdaq debut in June, which remains by far the largest IPO on record, having raised $85.7 billion. SpaceX had a larger market offering, though Anthropic's investor interest is running higher, the people said.

Retail enthusiasm is also running high. Vanda Research estimates Anthropic could see approximately $250 million of net retail buying on its first day of trading Thursday — double the size seen at SpaceX's IPO. Jai Malhi, senior strategist at Vanda, described it as "far and away the largest amount of net retail buying of any stock that we've seen in our data on the first day of its listing" in an interview with CNBC.

Anthropic's financials underpin much of that interest. For the nine months ended September 30, the company reported $33.24 billion in revenue, up roughly 1,200% from the same period a year earlier, according to its prospectus. The company disclosed a $104 billion net loss over that stretch, though it said most of that figure stemmed from changes in the fair value of convertible notes and derivative liabilities. Its run-rate revenue stood at approximately $81 billion at the end of October, up $10 billion from the previous month.

The listing arrives during a turbulent stretch for the broader AI sector. The market has absorbed concerns over rising interest rates, the affordability of data center debt financing, and last week's resignation of Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang — which came days after Huang acknowledged an affair with a businesswoman alleged to be a Chinese spy.

Tech's largest companies have deep financial exposure to Anthropic. Amazon and Google each hold stakes valued at over $100 billion. In April, Amazon agreed to invest up to $25 billion in the company, and Google announced plans the same month to invest up to $40 billion. A group that includes Google, Microsoft, Nvidia, and Advanced Micro Devices agreed collectively to invest $26 billion in the IPO.

Chipmaker Broadcom also has a significant role, having announced a special purpose vehicle to finance $63 billion in chips and networking products to be leased to Anthropic.

CEO Dario Amodei, who co-founded the company alongside other former OpenAI researchers less than six years ago, addressed the company's mission in its roadshow video. "I think if we do this right, we can create an age of miracles where humanity is able to solve problems that have eluded us for millennia," Amodei said. "We have to make sure that all the risks, all the ways that things can go wrong with this technology, we very carefully address those risks."

Anthropic's prospectus included more than 90 pages of risk factors. OpenAI, its primary competitor in the enterprise market, has confidentially filed for an IPO and has indicated a debut is likely in 2027.

How Anthropic prices and performs on its first trading day will serve as a closely watched gauge of investor appetite for large-scale AI businesses at a moment when the sector faces mounting questions about capital efficiency, governance, and the sustainability of its extraordinary growth trajectory.
