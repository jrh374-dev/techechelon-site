Finnish regulators have ordered Google to suspend construction on two data centers in the country, a move that complicates the tech company's plans for what has become one of Europe's most sought-after locations for AI infrastructure.

The Finnish Licensing and Supervision Agency, known as LVV, issued a notice on Tuesday to Google's subsidiary Tuike Finland Oy, directing it to halt work at projects planned for the cities of Muhos and Kajaani until environmental impact assessments have been completed. The suspension order carries a deadline of October 23.

The directive covers a broad range of ground-level activities, including the removal of trees, removal of topsoil, excavation, quarrying, crushing, transfer of soil masses, ditching, and the construction of roads and storage areas, according to a translation of the LVV's press release. Planning, measurements, and soil surveys are exempt from the order.

"In our opinion, the measures in question will change the environment and cause impacts, the identification and assessment of which are among the key objectives of the EIA procedure," said Tommi Muilu, head of the environmental department at the LVV, in the agency's statement.

Google acknowledged the regulatory action and said it would comply. "We understand the concerns and have fallen short of our own high standards in this instance," a Google spokesperson said. The spokesperson added that the company "acted in good faith under the Forestry Act, and ran nature surveys and put in place measures to protect high-value areas." Google also said it is advancing landscaping and biodiversity plans that include tree planting across 130 hectares at the Muhos site.

The order lands roughly a month after Google committed to investing $15 billion in AI infrastructure in Finland — its largest planned investment in Europe. The capital is set to be deployed through 2028 into data centers and supporting investments such as energy projects.

Finland has drawn intense interest from hyperscalers and data center developers amid the global AI build-out, attracting attention for its available land and electricity supply at a time when power is scarce across much of the continent. The concentration of activity has led some in the industry to dub the country the "Texas of Europe," a reference to the U.S. state's emergence as one of the leading destinations for large-scale AI computing facilities.

Several projects with potential capacities of hundreds of megawatts have recently been announced in Finland, underscoring the scale of demand that regulators are now being asked to weigh against environmental considerations.

The LVV's intervention reflects a broader pattern across Europe, where the pace of data center development is increasingly colliding with environmental permitting requirements. Similar tensions have surfaced in Denmark, where grid capacity has struggled to keep pace with surging demand from data center operators.

For Google, the Finnish halt is an early test of how its ambitious European AI infrastructure program will navigate the regulatory environment. The suspension applies only to physical construction activity and does not affect planning work — meaning the company can continue preparing for eventual development while the environmental assessments proceed. How quickly those assessments are completed will likely determine whether the October 23 deadline becomes a brief pause or a more prolonged interruption to the company's $15 billion timeline.
