Meta's advertising business expanded at more than twice the rate of Google's search division in the third quarter, according to earnings results from both companies, underscoring how artificial intelligence is producing divergent outcomes for two of the internet's dominant ad platforms.

Meta's ad revenue rose 26% year-over-year to $63.1 billion in the quarter. Google's Search and other unit posted 12% growth, bringing in $63.4 billion — putting the two businesses nearly equal in size by revenue, though Google collected an additional $11 billion from YouTube in the same period.

The 14-percentage-point growth gap is the widest in Meta's favor since 2021, reflecting what analysts describe as fundamentally different AI dynamics at each company.

For Meta, AI has translated directly into more precise ad targeting. The company's AI-powered business assistant, which advertisers can connect to its Ads Manager system, drove measurable results in the quarter. Meta CFO Susan Li said on the earnings call that advertisers who adopted the system's recommendations "drove almost 5% more conversions" and "increased their advertising investment by 9% compared with similar non-adopters." Li cited syrup producer Simply Organic Maple as an example, saying the company saw a 57% increase in purchases and a 36% decrease in cost per purchase after using the tool. Nearly 10 million advertisers used at least one Meta AI tool in the quarter, Li said.

Google's challenge is structural. As chatbots — including OpenAI's ChatGPT, Anthropic's Claude, and Meta's recently launched Muse assistant — become viable alternatives to traditional search, fewer users are clicking on the blue links that generate Google's ad revenue. Search query volume hit a record in the quarter, the company said, but revenue growth continued to slow.

"It's a double-edged sword, because as AI revolutionizes discovery, people might come across tools they like better than Google," Nate Elliott, an analyst covering AI at Emarketer, told CNBC. "If ChatGPT or Claude or Muse is better at helping them find what they're looking for, people's search habits might change for the first time in a generation. That's potentially catastrophic for Google's ad business."

Google's chief business officer Philipp Schindler said on the company's earnings call that it is deploying Gemini models "across our entire ads infrastructure," adding targeting tools and improving ad quality. But Google's own AI Overviews and AI Mode features — which surface summary responses rather than links — may be compounding the cannibalization problem. Analysts at UBS wrote in a note following Google's results that search cannibalization fears would come "right back to the forefront, made worse by META's ad strength this quarter and the lingering Muse/agent threat."

The earnings divergence rattled markets. Alphabet suffered its worst single-day stock decline on record following its third-quarter report, falling more than 12% and erasing approximately $500 billion in market capitalization. Meta's stock dropped 14% in October — its worst monthly performance since October 2022 — though that came after a 27% surge in September, the stock's best month since November of that year.

Both companies are investing heavily in AI infrastructure even as returns remain uncertain. Google is projecting up to $205 billion in capital expenditures this year. Meta has not disclosed a comparable annual figure but has signaled comparable ambition, with both companies now operating in negative free cash flow territory as spending outpaces near-term revenue gains.

Google does have a buffer that Meta lacks: its cloud computing division reported more than 100% growth to $31 billion in quarterly revenue, fueled by demand for AI infrastructure and its custom tensor processing units. Meta, which still depends on advertising for nearly all of its revenue, is working to build a subscription business around its AI models and agents.

How quickly chatbot adoption erodes Google's search dominance — and whether Meta's ad-AI flywheel continues to compound — will likely define the competitive landscape for online advertising well into 2027.
