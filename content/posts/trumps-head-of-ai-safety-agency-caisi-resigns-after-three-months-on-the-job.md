Chris Fall, the director of the Center for AI Standards and Innovation, has resigned from his position just three months after the Trump administration appointed him to lead the agency, raising fresh questions about continuity in U.S. AI policy oversight.

Fall was tapped to run CAISI in April. A Commerce Department spokesperson confirmed his departure Monday but did not disclose the reason for his exit.

Arvind Raman, the director of the National Institute of Standards and Technology, will serve as acting director of CAISI and continue to oversee the agency, Commerce Department spokesperson Kristen Eichamer said.

CAISI, housed within the U.S. Department of Commerce, is charged with helping the government facilitate "testing and collaborative research" around commercial artificial intelligence systems, according to its website.

Fall's departure compounds uncertainty over who serves as the Trump administration's central point person on AI policy and research. Venture capitalist David Sacks previously held the position of White House AI and crypto czar but stepped down from that role in March and has not yet been replaced.

The leadership vacuum comes at a sensitive moment for Washington. Chinese open-source models are gaining traction in the U.S. market against proprietary offerings from domestic developers. Late last week, Chinese startup Moonshot AI unveiled a new model, Kimi K3, which the company says closes the gap with leading U.S. systems and surpasses OpenAI and Anthropic's most capable models on some benchmarks.

The Trump administration has moved to assert greater control over the AI sector since the president signed an executive order on AI in June. That order asks developers to voluntarily provide models to the government for capability assessments ahead of full public releases and gave federal agencies 60 days to establish an evaluation framework.

The process has created friction for companies seeking to launch new models. OpenAI said in June that it agreed to limit the rollout of its GPT-5.6 model series to a group of "trusted partners" at the request of the U.S. government. Weeks earlier, Anthropic was required to disable access to its Fable 5 and Mythos 5 models to comply with a Commerce Department export control directive. Both companies later secured broader releases.

The administration has also announced a clearinghouse called "Gold Eagle," which aims to identify and remediate cybersecurity vulnerabilities and puts the White House in charge of approving which companies can access cutting-edge AI models. The clearinghouse has already begun to "intake and prioritize identified cybersecurity vulnerabilities" and "coordinate scanning verifications," according to a White House release. CAISI was not named as a participant in its development.

With Fall's exit, Raman will hold interim authority over CAISI's work even as the administration accelerates its push to shape how powerful AI systems are developed and deployed — a signal that the organizational structure underpinning that effort remains unsettled.
