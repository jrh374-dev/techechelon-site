UBS upgraded Box to buy from neutral on Thursday, lifting its price target on the content management company to $55 from $37 — implying 27% upside from Wednesday's close — citing deepening confidence that the company's artificial intelligence push can sustain long-term revenue growth.

Analyst Taylor McGinnis laid out the thesis in a note to clients Wednesday. "We upgrade Box to Buy from Neutral, as checks raise our conviction that the recent AI-driven growth acceleration can extend beyond the initial upgrade cycle and support sustained double-digit revs growth," McGinnis wrote.

The upgrade sent Box shares 3.6% higher in premarket trading Thursday.

At the core of UBS's argument is Box's positioning within what McGinnis calls the "enterprise content layer" — the layer of corporate infrastructure that houses large volumes of unstructured data. The bank argues that proximity to that data gives Box an advantage as enterprises shift toward AI-powered workflows.

"Investors have been looking for evidence that AI can drive an inflection in software growth, and Box is increasingly demonstrating exactly that," McGinnis wrote. "Its close positioning to unstructured data in the enterprise content layer is enabling it to move into higher-value AI-driven workflows."

McGinnis enumerated several use cases driving the expansion: client onboarding, loan underwriting, claims and fraud processing, contract analysis, insurance documentation, and HR and finance workflows.

UBS also revised its revenue growth forecast for Box upward, to 10% from 9% on a constant currency basis for fiscal 2028. McGinnis described that estimate as "a starting point that has upside potential," reinforcing the bank's view that Box's transformation from a content repository into an AI-powered workflow platform is still in early stages.

Wall Street remains divided on the stock. According to LSEG data, five analysts carry a buy or strong buy rating, four hold a neutral position, and one has a sell. Box shares have risen 44% year to date heading into Thursday's session.

The upgrade arrives amid broader market discussion about whether AI investment is translating into measurable software revenue growth — a question that has weighed on valuations across the enterprise software sector.

Palo Alto Networks added to that conversation Thursday, surging 10% in premarket trading after reporting fiscal first-quarter adjusted earnings of $1.05 per share on revenue of $3.44 billion, both above LSEG consensus estimates of 97 cents and $3.31 billion, respectively. The cybersecurity company issued full-year guidance calling for adjusted earnings of $4.26 to $4.30 per share on revenue between $14.52 billion and $14.57 billion, ahead of analyst expectations.

The dual moves in Box and Palo Alto Networks signal that selective software names with demonstrable AI revenue contributions are finding renewed favor with institutional analysts, even as the broader tech sector navigates uncertainty over AI valuations and rising yields.
